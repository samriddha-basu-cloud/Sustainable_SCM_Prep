Syllabus topic 5 📘. **Lecture 9 learning objectives** 📘: (1) identify technologies that can improve SC sustainability; (2) distinguish technology benefits from sustainability outcomes; (3) identify economic, environmental and social trade-offs; (4) evaluate technology adoption using a multi-dimensional framework; (5) apply it to real SC decisions; (6) understand why the same technology can produce different sustainability outcomes in different supply chains.

## Part A — Technology and sustainability (Lecture 9)

### 1. Technology ≠ sustainability 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
*If a company installs AI, IoT, blockchain and robots in its supply chain, does that automatically make the supply chain sustainable?* **No.**

| Technology **can reduce** | Technology **can increase** |
|---|---|
| Transport emissions | Energy consumption |
| Inventory | Electronic waste |
| Waste | Capital outlay |
| Worker-safety incidents (and improve traceability) | Cybersecurity risks; the CO₂ footprint of digital infrastructure |

**"Technology adoption → operational performance → sustainability performance is *not* a guaranteed linear relationship."** 📘

### 2. The fourth pillar: resilience 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Lecture 9 adds **Resilience** to the three pillars of sustainable network design:
| Environmental (Planet) | Social (People) | Economic (Profits) | **Resilience** (4th pillar) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO₂e, energy, water, pollution, circularity, consumption, waste | Safety, CSR, inclusion, diversity, human rights, data privacy | Cost, revenue, ROI, working capital, service level, productivity | **Visibility, agility, recovery, response, assurance** |

Linked to the **Carter–Rogers (2008) framework**: SSCM sits at the intersection of environmental, social and economic performance, supported by four facets — **risk management, transparency, strategy and culture**. Short form on the slide: *environmentally friendly + socially ethical + economically viable.*

### 3. The technology landscape — Supply Chain 4.0 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Pyramid, bottom to top ("from digital foundations to intelligent, sustainable supply chains")**:
1. **ERP / digital integration** (foundation)
2. **Automation / robotics / AGVs**
3. **Cloud / platforms**
4. **Blockchain / distributed ledger**
5. **IoT / sensors / RFID**
6. **Digital twins**
7. **Advanced analytics / ML**
8. **AI / GenAI** (top)

**Industry 4.0 technologies applied to each SC process**:
| Process | Technologies |
|---|---|
| Sourcing | AI, analytics, blockchain, supplier portals |
| Production | IoT, robotics, digital twins, AI/ML, predictive maintenance |
| Warehousing | IoT, automation, AGVs, analytics, WMS |

Goal: **smart, connected, transparent, sustainable, resilient.**

### 4. From cost optimisation to sustainability optimisation 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| SC problem | Technology | Potential benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Excess inventory | AI forecasting | Less waste |
| Empty trucks | AI route optimisation | Lower fuel / CO₂ |
| Product recalls | Blockchain | Faster traceability |
| Equipment failures | IoT + predictive maintenance | Less material / energy waste |
| Warehouse labour | Robotics | Productivity / safety |
| Food spoilage | IoT sensors | Less food waste |
| Supplier ESG monitoring | Digital platforms | Better transparency |
| Reverse logistics | RFID / IoT | Better recovery |
| Circular economy | Digital product passports | Material traceability |
| Scenario planning | Digital twins | Better network decisions |

**NOTE: potential benefit ≠ realised benefit** 📘.

### 5. The five hidden trade-offs 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| # | Trade-off | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Efficiency vs environmental footprint** | Electricity consumption, equipment manufacture, e-waste, capital intensity |
| 2 | **Traceability vs energy** | Blockchain architectures (especially proof-of-work) have a CO₂ footprint and high energy and resource needs |
| 3 | **Visibility vs privacy** | Cybersecurity risks, surveillance concerns, data-ownership issues |
| 4 | **Global optimisation vs local resilience** | A globally optimised, lean network is exposed to disruptions, geopolitical events, supplier failures, extreme weather |
| 5 | **Digital inclusion vs supplier exclusion** | MNCs with advanced systems vs small suppliers who cannot afford them |

**"Technology can improve Tier-1 sustainability while worsening Tier-2 / Tier-3 inclusion."** 📘

### 6. The technology–sustainability decision matrix 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
(The slide calls it a "4 × 4 matrix" but draws a 2 × 2 grid.)
| | **Low business value** | **High business value** |
|---|---|---|
| **High sustainability impact** | **Reconsider** | **Prioritise** |
| **Low sustainability impact** | **Avoid** | **Optimise** |

### 7. Technology Adoption Score and Net Technology Value 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- **TAS = 25% Economic + 25% Environmental + 20% Social + 15% Resilience + 15% Strategic fit**, each dimension scored **1–5**.
- **Risk adjustment — Net Technology Value = Gross benefits − Adoption cost − Lifecycle sustainability cost − Risk cost.**
- A 2026 study similarly lists **technology maturity, sustainability performance, emissions reduction and investment cost** among the key criteria for evaluating blockchain–IoT adoption.

### 8. Case: Walmart–IBM blockchain (international) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- **Problem**: food-borne contamination needs rapid source identification; traditional traceability took *days*.
- **Technology**: blockchain + IoT/data capture + QR-based identification; Walmart and IBM built a **Hyperledger Fabric**-based food-traceability system.
- **Result**: the mango pilot cut provenance tracing from **7 days → 2.2 seconds**.
- **Sustainability benefits**: economic (faster recalls, reduced damages, lower costs); environmental (less wastage); social (improved safety, consumer confidence); resilience (rapid contamination identification).
- **Trade-offs**: integration cost, supplier capacity, data quantity and quality, interoperability, governance, scalability.

### 9. Case: Asian Paints (Indian) 📘 ⭐⭐
Integrates **AI/ML, advanced analytics, IoT, digital twins, robotics and SC optimisation**. Technology helps reduce inventory, energy, water, operational inefficiency and manufacturing losses. Asian Paints reports its digital-manufacturing initiatives aim at **improving asset utilisation, reducing working capital, and reducing energy and water usage**.

### 10. The Tech-Sustain framework — a 7-step guide 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Define the problem** — what SC problem are we solving?
2. **Establish the baseline** — cost? CO₂? waste? service level? risk?
3. **Identify technology alternatives** — AI / IoT / blockchain / automation / digital twin / platform.
4. **Evaluate total lifecycle impact** — **capex + opex + energy + people + e-waste + cybersecurity.**
5. **Evaluate the triple bottom line** — economic + environmental + social.
6. **Stress-test unintended consequences** — ask *"What could become worse because of this technology?"*
7. **Pilot → measure → scale** — do not jump directly from proof of concept to enterprise-wide implementation.
*"Smart technology. Sustainable impact. Measured value."* and **"Don't digitise the problem — transform it sustainably."**

### 11. Recommended case portfolio (Lecture 9) 📘 ⭐⭐
| Case | Technology | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Walmart–IBM | Blockchain | Traceability vs cost/energy |
| Thai tuna | AI + blockchain | Data value vs complexity |
| Asian Paints | AI/ML + IoT + digital twin + robotics | Automation vs investment/energy |
| Tata Motors | Digital supplier platforms + sustainability technologies | Decarbonisation vs supplier capability |
| ITC e-Choupal | Digital platform | Technology sophistication vs inclusion |
| Indian agri-food blockchain | Blockchain | Transparency vs adoption cost |

### 12. Key takeaways (Lecture 9) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- Technology is an **enabler, not a sustainability strategy.**
- **Optimising one KPI can damage another.**
- Measure the **full technology life cycle**: manufacture → implementation → operation → maintenance → disposal.
- Digital supply chains require **digital ecosystems** (suppliers must come along).
- **The best technology is not the most advanced technology.**

### 13. The Navya exercise (summary) 📘
Navya Consumer Products (5 plants, 20 warehouses, 1,500 suppliers, 8,000 distributors, 50,000 outlets) considers PROJECT SUSTAIN_X (AI + IoT + blockchain): ₹80 crore capex, ₹12 crore a year opex; expected inventory −15%, transport −8%, waste −20%, forecast accuracy +15%. Recommended answer: **pilot**, because TAS is about 3.8 and NPV is positive only if about 87% or more of the benefits materialise; supplier inclusion is the main social risk. ## Part B — Quality management as a sustainability tool (Lecture 10, Section B)

### 14. Why quality is a sustainability issue 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**"Poor quality creates a costly chain"**: defects → rework → extra materials + energy → extra transportation → extra waste → **higher carbon footprint**. *Quality at the source prevents waste, saves resources and reduces carbon.*
**"Quality failure is simultaneously an economic and environmental failure."** This connects **TQM + Lean + Six Sigma + Green Supply Chain.** ([Numerical](p4-performance-measurement-numericals.md))

### 15. The seven ISO quality-management principles and their sustainability link 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
ISO identifies these as the foundation of ISO 9000/9001:
1. **Customer focus**
2. **Leadership**
3. **Engagement of people**
4. **Process approach** → *life-cycle thinking*
5. **Improvement** → *carbon and waste reduction*
6. **Evidence-based decision making** → *ESG data*
7. **Relationship management** → *sustainable suppliers*
*"Quality today. Sustainability tomorrow."*

### 16. Lean + Green + Quality: TIMWOOD 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
The seven lean wastes: **T**ransportation, **I**nventory, **M**otion, **W**aiting, **O**verproduction, **O**verprocessing, **D**efects. Sustainability view: **each waste potentially creates cost + energy + material + carbon + time.** Reducing defects gives less rework → less material → less energy → less waste → **less CO₂**.

### 17. Quality tools that serve sustainability ⭐⭐
The course names Six Sigma, TQM, Kaizen, Lean and ISO systems; standard tools you can cite:
- **PDCA (Deming cycle)** — the logic of ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 continual improvement.
- **DMAIC (Six Sigma)** — Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control; used for scrap, energy and yield projects.
- **Seven QC tools** — check sheet, Pareto chart (which 20% of sources cause 80% of waste or emissions), cause-and-effect (fishbone), histogram, scatter diagram, control chart (SPC on energy per unit), stratification/flow chart.
- **Poka-yoke** (mistake-proofing), **5S** (sort, set in order, shine, standardise, sustain; often extended with Safety), **TPM** (total productive maintenance; cuts breakdowns, leaks and energy waste), **value-stream mapping** (now "environmental VSM" with energy and water per step), **FMEA** (failure modes and effects, also for environmental aspects), **SMED** (quick changeover).
- **Cost of quality** (prevention, appraisal, internal failure, external failure) extended to a "carbon cost of poor quality".

### 18. Green quality management: ISO 14001 and the integrated management system 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- **ISO 14001** provides a framework for an **Environmental Management System (EMS)** and continual improvement of environmental performance (Plan–Do–Check–Act: environmental policy, aspects and impacts, legal compliance, objectives, operational control, monitoring, internal audit, management review).
- **The 2026 edition** emphasises environmental protection, business outcomes and integration with environmental priorities 📘.
- **Integrated Management System (IMS)**: **ISO 9001 (quality) + ISO 14001 (environment) + ISO 45001 (occupational health & safety)**, all built on the common high-level structure (Annex SL), so one set of audits and reviews covers all three.
- *"ESG reporting: disclosure = integrated sustainable operations."*

## Quick exam answers (CO6 3-markers) ⭐⭐⭐
**Q1. "Technology does not automatically make a supply chain sustainable." Explain.**
Technology can cut transport emissions, inventory and waste, but it also adds energy use, e-waste, capital outlay, cyber risk and a digital-infrastructure footprint. The chain technology → operational performance → sustainability is not linear; benefits must be measured over the full technology life cycle, and potential benefit ≠ realised benefit.

**Q2. List the five hidden trade-offs of technology adoption.**
Efficiency vs environmental footprint; traceability vs energy (blockchain); visibility vs privacy; global optimisation vs local resilience; digital inclusion vs supplier exclusion (Tier-1 gains, Tier-2/3 excluded).

**Q3. State the TAS formula and Net Technology Value.**
TAS = 0.25 Economic + 0.25 Environmental + 0.20 Social + 0.15 Resilience + 0.15 Strategic fit (each 1–5). NTV = Gross benefits − Adoption cost − Lifecycle sustainability cost − Risk cost.

**Q4. Why is quality a sustainability issue?**
Defects cause rework, extra materials and energy, extra transport and waste, so they raise carbon. Quality at source prevents waste, saves resources and cuts CO₂: quality failure is both an economic and an environmental failure (TQM + Lean + Six Sigma + green SCM).

**Q5. Explain the Tech-Sustain framework.**
Define the problem → establish a baseline (cost, CO₂, waste, service, risk) → identify alternatives → evaluate total lifecycle impact (capex, opex, energy, people, e-waste, cyber) → evaluate TBL → stress-test unintended consequences → pilot, measure, scale.
