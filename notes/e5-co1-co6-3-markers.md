## CO1: SSCM, strategy and business performance (Lectures 1–3)

**Q1. Define Sustainable Supply Chain Management (SSCM).**
SSCM is the management of material, information and capital flows, and of co-operation among firms along the supply chain, while integrating **economic, environmental and social** goals derived from customer and stakeholder requirements (Seuring & Müller). It (1) extends SCM from cradle-to-grave to **cradle-to-cradle**, (2) adds carbon, waste and labour practices to cost, quality and speed, and (3) covers Scope 1, 2 and 3 across the whole chain. *Example:* Mahindra's carbon-neutrality goal with RE, EV ecosystem and green logistics.

**Q2. Differentiate traditional SCM and SSCM.**
| | Traditional SCM | SSCM |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Minimise cost, maximise efficiency | Balance economic, environmental, social value |
| Flow and horizon | Linear, short-term | Circular (closed loop), long-term |
| Suppliers and stakeholders | Transactional, price-based; shareholders and customers | Collaborative, development-based; all stakeholders |
Lecture 1: *"Cheapest supplier wins → ethical supplier wins; waste is unavoidable → waste = design failure."*

**Q3. Explain the Triple Bottom Line with an example.**
Elkington (1994): measure a firm on **People, Planet and Profit**. (1) **Planet**: energy, water, packaging waste, fuel use. (2) **People**: labour welfare, supplier ethics, safety, diversity (gig delivery workers). (3) **Profit**: lower waste and fuel cost, ESG investment, brand value. *Example:* Flipkart's last-mile EVs and recyclable packaging cut emissions (planet), fuel cost (profit) and improve rider conditions (people).

**Q4. Explain the sustainable supply chain framework of Lecture 1.**
Each stage gets a "+" practice: (1) **Procurement**: ethical and local sourcing, supplier audits; (2) **Manufacture**: renewable energy, lean, waste minimisation; (3) **Logistics and warehousing**: EV fleets, route optimisation, multimodal, rooftop solar, natural lighting; plus (4) **Reverse logistics**: returns, recycling, circular economy. Hook: *"Procure ethically, make lean and clean, move smart, store green, bring it back."*

**Q5. Linear vs circular supply chain (cradle-to-grave vs cradle-to-cradle).**
Linear = take → make → use → dispose; responsibility ends at sale. Circular = products return via **reverse logistics** to reuse, repair, remanufacture and recycle, so end-of-life becomes new input. Three enablers: design for disassembly, collection networks, recovery facilities. *Example:* Attero and Lohum recover lithium, cobalt and nickel from EV batteries under India's **EPR** rules.

**Q6. Why is SSCM strategy important today?**
(1) **Regulatory pressure**: BRSR (SEBI), EU **CBAM**, India's CCTS carbon market, EPR, EU due-diligence law. (2) **Investor pressure**: ESG ratings drive the cost of capital. (3) **Customer pressure and operational benefits**: B2B buyers audit suppliers; energy, waste and logistics savings and resilience. Lecture 2: a chain that is profitable today can become *"illegal, expensive or unacceptable tomorrow."*

**Q7. What are the risks of ignoring sustainability in the supply chain?**
(1) **Export barriers and carbon costs**: CBAM, carbon taxes, EU deforestation rules. (2) **Supply disruptions**: floods, heat, water scarcity, resource shortages. (3) **Reputation, investor and customer loss**: boycotts, lower ESG ratings, lost B2B contracts. *Example:* an Indian steel or aluminium exporter without verified emissions data pays CBAM on punitive default values.

**Q8. List the five steps of SSCM strategy development.**
**Assess** the current state (carbon footprint, network, supplier performance, waste, energy) → **analyse stakeholders** → **develop future scenarios** (carbon prices, disruptions, energy cost, water) → **design** the strategy (green sourcing, RE, circularity, supplier development, network redesign) → **operationalise** (projects, KPIs, budgets, owners). *"Understand stakeholders → anticipate change → design the response → execute for impact."*

**Q9. What are the ingredients of an SSCM strategy, and how does it cascade?**
Ingredients: business **vision**, **stakeholder expectations**, **resources**, **capabilities**, **supply-chain design** and **sustainability objectives**. It cascades corporate → business → supply-chain → operations and logistics strategy, and at each level must serve **People, Planet, Profit**. *"Supply chain strategy must support overall business strategy."*

**Q10. Explain stakeholder mapping for a sustainable supply chain.**
Identify **internal** (employees, management, shareholders) and **external** stakeholders (customers, suppliers, regulators, communities, NGOs and media, investors and lenders), then place them on a **power–interest grid**: manage closely (regulators, key customers, investors), keep satisfied, keep informed (communities, NGOs), monitor. *"Successful supply chains don't just deliver products; they deliver value to stakeholders."*

**Q11. What is a Sustainability Balanced Scorecard? Give one KPI per perspective.**
Kaplan & Norton's scorecard with sustainability KPIs, which *"converts sustainability into action"*: **Financial**: energy savings, ROI of sustainability projects · **Customer**: sustainable product sales, ESG rating · **Internal process**: carbon emissions, water use, transport efficiency · **Learning & growth**: employee training, innovation projects, sustainability culture.

**Q12. How does SSCM improve business performance?**
(1) **Cost**: less energy, waste and fuel (lean = green). (2) **Revenue and market access**: green products, B2B and export eligibility (CBAM, buyer audits). (3) **Risk and capital**: fewer disruptions, better ESG ratings, cheaper capital, brand and talent. *Example:* ITC eChoupal cut intermediation cost for farmers and ITC while improving rural incomes. Measured through TBL KPIs and a sustainability BSC.

**Q13. Name the five performance dimensions of Lecture 3 (Q + D + I + R + S).**
**Quality** (defect PPM, returns, warranty claims) · **Delivery** (on-time %, lead time, reliability) · **Innovation** (new products, R&D, patents, automation) · **Resilience** (disruptions, recovery time, supplier diversity) · **Sustainability** (carbon, energy, water, worker safety, ethical sourcing %). *"High-performing supply chains integrate Q + D + I + R + S."*

**Q14. Why measure sustainable performance? (Lecture 3)**
*"If you cannot measure it, you cannot improve it"*; *"performance measurement is a core enabler, not an optional one."* It (1) drives **accountability** and better decisions (KPIs with targets and owners), (2) builds **shareholder and stakeholder trust** with evidence for disclosure (BRSR) and protection against greenwashing, (3) improves **resilience and competitive advantage** through benchmarking and continuous improvement. Use **SMART** KPIs.

**Q15. Give the four types of Indian SSCM cases from Lectures 1–2.**
(1) **Delivery-based**: ITC e-Choupal (direct farmer link, price transparency), HUL sustainable sourcing. (2) **Recovery-based**: EV-battery circular chain (Tata Autocomp, Attero, Lohum, EPR). (3) **Reduction-based**: Hindustan Zinc + GreenLine LNG/EV trucks (₹400 crore), Mahindra carbon neutrality, Flipkart last-mile EVs. Takeaway: *"tomorrow supply chains will compete on sustainability and resilience."*

**Q16. What are the challenges of measuring sustainability performance? (Lecture 3)**
(1) **Data**: lack of supplier transparency (Tier-2/3), Scope 3 complexity, data quality and verification. (2) **Comparability**: no standardised KPIs, multiple stakeholders with different expectations. (3) **Cost and short-termism**: high effort of data collection and reporting, short-term financial pressure. *Enablers:* AI, IoT sensors, blockchain traceability, cloud platforms, big-data analytics, digital twins.

## CO6: Technology adoption and SSCM strategy (Lectures 9 and 11)

**Q17. "Technology does not automatically make a supply chain sustainable." Explain.**
Technology can cut transport, inventory and waste, but it also brings (1) **its own footprint**: device manufacture, e-waste, data-centre and blockchain energy; (2) **capital, cyber and dependence risks**; (3) **social exclusion** of small suppliers who cannot adopt it. Potential benefit ≠ realised benefit, so evaluate over the **technology life cycle** (Lecture 9). *"Technology is an enabler, not a sustainability strategy."*

**Q18. List the five hidden trade-offs of technology adoption.**
(1) Efficiency vs environmental footprint; (2) **traceability vs energy** (blockchain); (3) visibility vs privacy; (4) global optimisation vs local resilience; (5) **digital inclusion vs supplier exclusion** (Tier-1 gains, Tier-2/3 left out). *Example:* grid-charged e-trucks in India can emit more than diesel.

**Q19. Why is resilience called the fourth pillar of sustainability?**
A chain that collapses in a flood, pandemic or sanctions shock cannot deliver economic, social or environmental value. (1) Resilience protects all three TBL pillars; (2) technology choices (single cloud vendor, single-source chips) can create new fragility; (3) so TAS gives **resilience 15%** weight. *Example:* COVID and Red Sea disruptions.

**Q20. Explain the technology–sustainability decision matrix.**
Two axes: business value and sustainability impact. **High value + high impact → Prioritise**; **low value + high impact → Reconsider** (redesign or seek subsidy); **high value + low impact → Optimise** (reduce its footprint); **low + low → Avoid**. *Example:* AI route optimisation (fuel and CO₂ savings) is "Prioritise".

**Q21. State the TAS formula and the Net Technology Value.**
**TAS = 0.25 Economic + 0.25 Environmental + 0.20 Social + 0.15 Resilience + 0.15 Strategic fit** (each 1–5). **NTV = Gross benefits − Adoption cost − Lifecycle sustainability cost − Risk cost.** Use both: TAS for multi-criteria fit, NTV for risk-adjusted money. Navya case: TAS 3.8, positive but sensitive NPV → **pilot**.

**Q22. Explain the Tech-Sustain framework (7 steps).**
Define the problem → establish a **baseline** (cost, CO₂, waste, service, risk) → identify alternatives → evaluate **total lifecycle impact** (capex, opex, energy, people, e-waste, cyber) → evaluate TBL → **stress-test** unintended consequences → **pilot, measure, scale**. *"Don't digitise the problem; transform it sustainably."*

**Q23. Give one technology and its sustainability use for each of IoT, AI, digital twin and blockchain.**
**IoT**: real-time energy, cold-chain and emissions monitoring. **AI**: demand forecasting and route optimisation (less waste and fewer km). **Digital twin**: simulate network or plant changes before building. **Blockchain**: traceability (Walmart–IBM mango tracing cut from **7 days to 2.2 seconds**). Each needs its own energy and data accounted for.

**Q24. What is the "polycrisis" facing supply chains?**
Six simultaneous pressures (Lecture 11): **climate change** (extreme weather), **geopolitical fragmentation** (sanctions, protectionism, McKinsey's shifting "geometry of trade"), **critical-resource constraints** (lithium, cobalt, rare earths), **regulatory pressure** (carbon disclosure, due diligence, EPR, DPP), **social risks** (forced labour, living wages), **technology disruption** (AI, automation). Chains must be green *and* resilient at once.

**Q25. Distinguish climate mitigation and adaptation in supply chains.**
**Mitigation** reduces the chain's impact on the climate: RE, efficiency, modal shift, low-carbon materials. **Adaptation** reduces the chain's vulnerability to climate change: climate-risk siting, diversified suppliers, buffers, flood-proof warehouses. The best strategy does **both**. *Example:* new location questions: climate risk, future water, grid carbon intensity, single-port dependence.

**Q26. Explain the 7-step SSCM strategy architecture (D-P-R-C-M-I-S).**
**Diagnose** hotspots (material, carbon, water, social, risk) → **Prioritise** high-impact activities → **Redesign** products, suppliers, processes, logistics, network → **Collaborate** with suppliers, customers, government, competitors → **Measure** carbon, circularity, cost, service, social → **Innovate** (digital, clean tech, business models) → **Scale** into procurement, investment and governance.

**Q27. "Do not outsource sustainability." Explain.**
Most emissions and human-rights risks sit with suppliers; if the buyer's practices (price squeezes, last-minute orders, short lead times) push suppliers to cut corners, the buyer cannot claim a sustainable chain. So (1) use **balanced procurement KPIs** (cost + kg CO₂e/unit + RE % + recycled content + safety + human rights + single-source dependency + traceability), (2) **develop** suppliers rather than only audit them, (3) link ESG scores to volume. *Example:* Walmart Project Gigaton, Apple's supplier clean-energy programme.

**Q28. "Green transport ≠ sustainable logistics." Explain.**
Switching to EVs or LNG trucks helps, but logistics impact also depends on (1) **packaging and cube** (utilisation), (2) **delivery frequency and empty miles**, (3) **warehousing, inventory and returns**. *"The cleanest kilometre is often the kilometre eliminated"* (Lecture 11). *Example:* raising utilisation from 62% to 80% and cutting empty km to 10% saves ~29% CO₂ with the same diesel fleet.

**Q29. "Circularity is a network redesign strategy, not just waste management." Explain.**
A circular chain adds **new flows and nodes**: collection, inspection and sorting, repair, refurbish, remanufacture and recycling, back to suppliers. These need different facilities, transport, skills, inventory and information systems, and products designed for disassembly. *Example:* Renault Refactory, Tata Motors Re.Wi.Re vehicle scrapping, IKEA buy-back.

**Q30. List the eight sustainability transitions (Lecture 11).**
Fossil → **renewable energy** · virgin → **circular materials** · linear → **circular products** · global concentration → **resilient diversification** · opaque → **traceable chains** · ownership → **access/service** (PaaS) · reactive → **predictive/AI** management · reporting → sustainability **embedded in decisions**. *"Bigger than going green."*

**Q31. What is the critical-minerals paradox?**
Decarbonisation (EVs, batteries, solar, wind, grids) **multiplies demand** for lithium, copper, nickel, cobalt, graphite and rare earths, but their extraction brings **water stress, biodiversity loss, emissions, child labour (cobalt, DRC) and geopolitical concentration** (China in processing). Responses: recycling (Lohum, Redwood), diversification, traceability (battery passport), material-efficient design.

**Q32. Explain JIT + JIC + JIS.**
**Just-in-time** (lean flow, low inventory) combined with **just-in-case** (strategic buffers, dual sourcing for resilience) and **just-in-sequence** (delivery in assembly order), inside **regional, diversified, digital, circular, low-carbon** networks optimised for **lowest life-cycle cost and risk**, not lowest purchase price. Lesson from COVID and the Red Sea crisis.

**Q33. What is the Digital Product Passport (DPP)?**
A digital record attached to a product (via QR or RFID) carrying its **materials, carbon footprint, repairability and end-of-life** information, required under the EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, **batteries first**. It (1) enables traceability, (2) supports repair and recycling, (3) makes claims verifiable. *"Future supply chain = physical supply chain + digital information layer."*

**Q34. Give three Indian and three international SSCM best-practice cases.**
*India:* **ITC eChoupal** (digital farmer procurement), **Tata Motors Re.Wi.Re** (vehicle recycling), **Hindustan Zinc + GreenLine** (LNG/EV trucks); also HUL, Asian Paints (~60% RE, zero liquid discharge), Dalmia Cement. *International:* **Apple** (supplier RE: 17.8 GW; GHG −60% vs 2015), **Walmart Project Gigaton** (1 billion t of supplier emissions avoided by 2030), **Unilever** (sustainable crop sourcing), plus IKEA and Renault Refactory.
