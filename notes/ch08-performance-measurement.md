## 1. What should a sustainable supply chain optimise? 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
*"Can a supply chain be green but economically unsustainable?"* Yes — the slide's examples of one-sided optimisation:
- Very low carbon but prohibitively expensive.
- Low cost but poor labour conditions.
- High recycling rate but poor product quality.
- Local sourcing but higher emissions.
- Fast delivery but excessive inventory or air freight.

**Six performance dimensions and their typical question** 📘:
| Dimension | Typical question |
|---|---|
| Economic | Is the supply chain profitable? |
| Operational | Is it reliable and responsive? |
| Environmental | What is its ecological footprint? |
| Social | How does it affect workers and communities? |
| Governance | Is it ethical, transparent and controlled? |
| Resilience | Can it withstand disruption? |

**Key message: "Sustainability should not be treated as a separate department. It should become a performance dimension of the supply chain itself."** 📘

## 2. What is supply-chain sustainability performance? 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Performance = actual outcome compared with the desired target.**

A useful hierarchy: **Inputs** (energy, materials, labour, capital) → **Processes** (procurement, production, logistics, distribution) → **Outputs** (products, services, waste, emissions) → **Outcomes** (profitability, customer value, environmental impact, social impact, risk and resilience), with **continuous improvement** feeding back: identify material issues → measure baseline and KPIs → set targets → improve (implement actions) → verify (data quality and assurance) → report (disclose performance) → review (management decisions).

**"Do not confuse Activity → Output → Outcome → Impact."** 📘 Example: *"100% of suppliers trained in ESG is an activity, not proof of sustainability improvement."* The proof is the outcome (supplier emissions down, violations down).

Lecture 3's version: **inputs → processes → outputs → outcomes → business value**, ensuring *overall sustainable performance*.

## 3. Why measure sustainable performance? (Lecture 3) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- **Traditional vs sustainable performance**: traditional measures profit, revenue and cost for shareholders over the short term; sustainable performance adds environmental and social outcomes for all stakeholders over the long term.
- Comparing two companies: one with higher sales, profits and pay but higher debt; the other with strong profits, safe workplaces, lower emissions and ethical suppliers — the second is the sustainable performer.
- **"If you cannot measure it, you cannot improve it."** **"Performance measurement is a core enabler, not an optional one."**
- Benefits: drives **accountability**, supports **better decision-making**, builds **shareholder trust**, improves **resilience**, creates **competitive advantage**.

## 4. The KPI architecture (Lecture 10) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
*"From KPIs to impact: measure, manage, improve."*

| Level 1 — Economic | Level 2 — Operational | Level 3 — Environmental | Level 4 — Social | Level 5 — Governance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SC cost / sales | OTIF | Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions | Lost-time injury rate | Supplier code compliance |
| Total cost of ownership | Lead time | CO₂e per product | Employee turnover | Audit closure rate |
| Procurement savings | Forecast accuracy | Energy per unit | Living-wage coverage | Ethics violations |
| Inventory carrying cost | Perfect-order rate | Water per unit | Supplier labour violations | ESG data assurance |
| Cost of poor quality | Inventory turnover | Waste per unit | Gender diversity | Traceability |
| Return on SC assets | Supplier reliability | % recycled material | Human-rights assessments | |

*"Measure what matters. Manage consistently. Improve continuously."*

### The KPI trap 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
*"More KPIs ≠ better performance management."* A company may measure **75 sustainability indicators, 40 supplier indicators and 30 logistics indicators**, *"yet managers still cannot answer: are we becoming more sustainable?"*

**KPIs must be SMART** (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound), and **a KPI must answer four questions** 📘:
1. **What happened?**
2. **Why did it happen?**
3. **Who owns it?**
4. **What decision will change because of it?**

### Leading vs lagging indicators 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Leading indicators | Lagging indicators |
|---|---|
| Supplier ESG assessments | Actual supplier incidents |
| Employee training | Injury rate |
| Renewable-energy contract | Actual Scope 2 emissions |
| Eco-design projects | Product carbon footprint |
| Preventive maintenance | Equipment failure |
| Supplier development | Defect rate |
| Risk assessments | Disruption losses |
**"Lagging KPIs tell you what happened; leading KPIs tell you whether you are likely to improve."** 📘

### Supplier Sustainability Scorecard (Lecture 10) 📘 ⭐⭐
Dimensions: cost competitiveness, quality, delivery, environmental performance, labour & human rights, carbon reduction, governance, innovation (total 100). The question on the slide: *should a supplier scoring 95/100 on cost-quality-delivery but with serious labour violations be retained?* Answer: **no (or only under a strict corrective-action plan)** — a human-rights violation is a **knock-out criterion** that no weighted total can offset. *"This is where sustainability becomes a management decision rather than a reporting exercise."* ([Worked](p1-supplier-selection-numericals.md))

## 5. Performance dimensions and SMART KPIs (Lecture 3) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Q + D + I + R + S** across procurement → manufacturing → warehousing → transportation → distribution → customer:
- **Quality** (meet or exceed expectations): defect rate (PPM, %), complaints/returns, warranty claims, Six Sigma level, supplier performance.
- **Delivery** (right product, time, place, customer): on-time delivery %, order-fill cycle time, lead time, delivery reliability, transport efficiency.
- **Innovation** (improve experience): number of new products, R&D spend, digital technology, patents, automation level.
- **Resilience** (prepare for disruptions): supply disruptions, recovery time, supplier diversity, inventory risk, business continuity.
- **Sustainability** (long-term ESG value): carbon emissions, energy consumed, water consumed, worker safety, ethical sourcing %.

SMART KPI examples (Lecture 3): **economic** — cost per shipment, inventory days, % profitability; **environmental** — CO₂ per tonne-km, % material recycled, water intensity; **social** — accident frequency, % diversity, supplier audit score.

## 6. The performance dashboard (Lecture 3) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Six perspectives × four KPIs: **economic** (revenue growth, operating margin, cost per shipment, ROCE), **environmental** (CO₂ per t-km, energy intensity, water intensity, waste recycling rate), **social** (LTIFR, training hours, diversity, supplier audit score), **risk** (high-risk suppliers %, disruption incidents, risk assessments on time, risk-exposure score), **customer** (on-time delivery, perfect order, fill rate, NPS), **innovation** (new products, improvement projects, automation coverage, R&D % of revenue). *"Balanced dashboard = performance data → strategic decisions."* *"Track what matters. Take action. Drive sustainable performance across every dimension."* ([Reading and indexing it](p4-performance-measurement-numericals.md))

## 7. Benchmarking (Lecture 3) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
*Comparing an organisation's performance, processes and practices with the best-performing organisations or recognised standards to identify gaps and opportunities.*
| Type | Compare on | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Industry leaders** | SC efficiency, sustainability initiatives, cost optimisation, innovation and digitalisation | Amazon (logistics), Toyota (lean), Unilever (sustainable sourcing) |
| **Competitors** | Market share, product quality, cost competitiveness, ESG performance | Tata Motors vs M&M; Asian Paints vs Berger; HUL vs ITC |
| **Global standards** | Certification and disclosure | ISO 9001 (quality), ISO 14001 (environment), ISO 45001 (OH&S), ISO 27001 (data security); BRSR, GRI, CDP, SBTi |
| **Historical (internal)** | Own trend | Revenue growth, % profitability, carbon emissions, customer-satisfaction index, safety |
*"Benchmark not to imitate competition but to continuously outperform self."*

## 8. Challenges in sustainability performance measurement (Lecture 3) ⭐⭐⭐
Lack of supplier transparency (Tier-2/3 data), multiple stakeholders with different expectations, **lack of standardised KPIs** (hard to compare), high cost and effort of data collection and reporting, **Scope 3 complexity**, data quality and verification, short-term financial pressure. **Enablers**: AI, IoT sensors, blockchain traceability, cloud platforms, big-data analytics and digital twins.

**Emerging performance metrics** 📘: circularity, Scope 3 emissions, supplier ESG score, biodiversity, water risk, climate risk, **AI readiness**.

**Indian case list (Lecture 3)** 📘: Tata Steel (integrated sustainability KPIs), ITC (water-positive and carbon-positive operations), HUL (sustainable sourcing and plastic circularity), Mahindra Group (ESG performance management), Asian Paints (digital SC performance), Amul (inclusive rural SC performance), Tata Motors (supplier sustainability scorecards), JSW Steel (carbon-intensity reduction), Infosys (ESG dashboards and reporting), DCM Shriram (resource efficiency in manufacturing).

## Section C — Sustainability reporting (Lecture 10)

### 9. Why do companies report sustainability? 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Stakeholders demand information: **investors, customers, employees, regulators, banks, suppliers, communities**. Reporting serves **trust, risk management, capital allocation and accountability**. *"But reporting creates a critical problem: what should be measured, and what should be disclosed?"*

### 10. The reporting landscape — "different lenses, one goal: credible sustainability performance" 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Framework | Focus / content |
|---|---|
| **CDP** (Carbon Disclosure Project) | Questionnaires on climate change, water security, forests and deforestation, plastics and biodiversity, supply-chain management |
| **GRI 305** (Global Reporting Initiative) | Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions; GHG intensity; GHG reductions; ozone-depleting substances; NOx, SOx and other air emissions. GRI reports **impacts on the world** (impact materiality). |
| **TCFD** | Four pillars: **governance** (climate risks and opportunities), **strategy** (actual and potential impacts), **risk management** (identify, assess, manage), **metrics and targets** |
| **Integrated Reporting** (<IR>) | **Six capitals**: financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social & relationship, natural; content elements: organisation overview, business model, governance, risks, performance |
| **ISSB / IFRS S1–S2** | Global baseline for **investor-relevant** sustainability risks and opportunities; S1 general requirements, S2 climate (built on TCFD) |
| **BRSR** (India, SEBI) | **9 principles** (NGRBC), ~140 parameters; **Section A** general disclosure, **Section B** management & process disclosure, **Section C** principle-wise performance disclosure; P2 sustainable products, P6 environment (emissions, energy, waste) |

**Which question each answers (Lecture 10 takeaway)** 📘: **GRI → impacts; ISSB → investor-relevant sustainability risks/opportunities; BRSR → Indian regulatory ESG reporting.**

### 11. BRSR Core — from disclosure to credibility 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- SEBI introduced **BRSR Core** as a subset of key performance indicators across **nine ESG attributes** (e.g. GHG footprint, water footprint, energy footprint, waste management, employee well-being and safety, gender diversity, inclusive development, fairness with customers, openness of business), requiring **reasonable assurance**.
- The original 2023 glide path extended requirements progressively from the **top 150 listed entities in FY2023–24** toward the **top 1,000**.
- Traditional reporting says *"We are committed to sustainability"*; BRSR Core says *"Show me the data."*
- Managerial implications: **data ownership, internal controls, measurement methodology, supplier data, audit/assessment readiness, evidence trails.**
- *"BRSR mandate: Don't just report sustainability. Prove it."*

### 12. Company A vs Company B — report vs performance 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Company A: excellent report, beautiful graphics, ambitious targets, strong narrative. Company B: less attractive report but energy ↓25%, CO₂/unit ↓30%, water/unit ↓20%, supplier violations ↓40%, waste-to-landfill ↓60%. **"The report is not the performance. Measure performance first, report performance later."** ([Value of B's gains](p4-performance-measurement-numericals.md))

### 13. Greenwashing vs greenhushing 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Greenwashing | Greenhushing |
|---|---|
| **Overstating sustainability performance without adequate evidence** | **Under-communicating genuine sustainability achievements** |
| Examples: "eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable", "carbon neutral", "100% eco-friendly" with little evidence | Firms stay silent to avoid scrutiny, so stakeholders cannot reward real progress |
**Good reporting includes** 📘: **claim, metric, boundary, methodology, baseline, target, evidence.** Key message: *credible sustainability communication.* (India's CCPA Guidelines for the Prevention and Regulation of Greenwashing, 2024, and the EU Green Claims rules now penalise vague claims.)

## Section D — Case studies (Lecture 10) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Unilever (international)**: its 2025 annual report states it sourced **81% of key crops sustainably** (2030 target **95%**) and reported **more than 95% of purchase volumes of specified commodities as deforestation-free**. Possible scorecard: % sustainably sourced crops (sourcing transition), % traceable commodities (SC visibility), Scope 3 emissions (climate), farmer income (social impact), deforestation-free volume (nature), supplier compliance (governance), commodity supply risk (resilience). **Key lesson: agricultural sustainability is fundamentally a supply-chain problem.**

**Mahindra & Mahindra (Indian)**: 2025–26 reporting shows a strong focus on supplier ESG management. The supplier code of conduct was signed by **95% of suppliers by purchase value**, while cumulative environmental due diligence since FY2024 covered **68.1% of suppliers by value**. **Key lesson: is supplier ESG assessment enough — or should supplier ESG performance affect purchasing decisions?** (Answer: it must feed into sourcing decisions, volume allocation and supplier development; signing a code is an activity, not an outcome.)

## 16. The sustainable supply-chain performance loop 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Identify** — material sustainability issues.
2. **Measure** — baseline + KPIs.
3. **Target** — science, business or regulatory targets.
4. **Improve** — projects + supplier engagement.
5. **Verify** — data controls + assurance.
6. **Report** — BRSR / GRI / ISSB / annual report.
7. **Review** — management decisions.
*"Identify. Measure. Improve. Verify. Report. Repeat."*

## 17. Five tests for a good SSCM measurement system 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Material** | Does it measure what matters? |
| **Balanced** | Economic + operational + environmental + social? |
| **Actionable** | Can managers influence the KPI? |
| **Comparable** | Across time, suppliers and competitors? |
| **Credible** | Can the number be verified? |
**"If a KPI does not change a decision, question why it exists."**

## 18. Key takeaways (Lecture 10) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- Sustainability must become part of **core supply-chain performance management**.
- A good measurement system balances **cost + quality + service + carbon + people + risk**.
- Quality management and sustainability are interconnected: **waste + defects + resource efficiency + continuous improvement**.
- Reporting frameworks answer different questions: **GRI — impacts; ISSB — investor-relevant risks and opportunities; BRSR — Indian regulatory ESG reporting**.
- **Scope 3 makes supply-chain collaboration essential.**
- **Sector determines the sustainability hotspot.**

## Quick exam answers ⭐⭐⭐
**Q1. Distinguish leading and lagging indicators with two examples each.**
Lagging indicators report results after the fact (injury rate, actual Scope 2 emissions); leading indicators predict future results and can be acted on now (employee safety training, renewable-energy contracts). A good system pairs each lagging KPI with the leading KPIs that drive it.

**Q2. What is the KPI trap and how do you avoid it?**
Tracking too many indicators (75 + 40 + 30) without being able to say whether the chain is becoming more sustainable. Avoid it with a small, balanced, SMART set where each KPI answers what happened, why, who owns it and what decision will change, and passes the five tests.

**Q3. Compare GRI, TCFD/ISSB and BRSR.**
GRI: impact reporting for all stakeholders (GRI 305 for emissions). TCFD/ISSB: investor-focused climate and sustainability risks and opportunities (governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets). BRSR: SEBI-mandated Indian disclosure on 9 principles (Sections A–C), with BRSR Core requiring assured KPIs.

**Q4. Greenwashing vs greenhushing.**
Greenwashing overstates performance without evidence ("100% eco-friendly"); greenhushing under-communicates real achievements. Credible reporting states the claim, metric, boundary, methodology, baseline, target and evidence.

**Q5. List the seven steps of the SSCM performance loop.**
Identify → measure → target → improve → verify → report → review.
