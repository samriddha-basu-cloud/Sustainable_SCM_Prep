# CO3 · 10-Mark Numericals: Carbon Accounting (Scope 1, 2, 3)
Your teacher's pattern: **CO3 = 10 marks, a numerical on carbon accounting**. This page has five full 10-mark questions in the format you will see, each with a **marks split** and a **model answer written the way you should write it in the paper**. Q1 is the course's own case (carbon deck, slides 17–19) 📘. Q2–Q5 are practice questions in the same style 📗, built on the course's emission factors. Turn on **Self-test** (`T`) to hide the answers.

Full theory and more problems: [Carbon Accounting Numericals](p2-carbon-accounting-numericals.md) · [LCA & Carbon Footprints](ch02-lca-carbon-footprints.md).

---

## How a 10-mark carbon answer earns its marks ⭐⭐⭐
| Step | What to write | Marks it usually carries |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Classify | A table: source → Scope 1, 2 or 3 (and the Scope 3 category number) | 1–2 |
| 2. Formula | **Emissions = Activity data × Emission factor**; **CO₂e = mass of gas × GWP** | 1 |
| 3. Calculate | One line per source with units; convert kg → t (÷ 1,000) | 3–4 |
| 4. Summarise | Scope totals, grand total, **% share** of each scope, **intensity** (per unit, per tonne or per ₹ crore) | 1–2 |
| 5. Interpret + recommend | Biggest hotspot, two or three levers with the t CO₂e each one saves, one line on reporting (BRSR, GHG Protocol, MRV) | 2 |

**Factors to memorise (course 📘):** diesel **2.68 kg CO₂/l** · natural gas **1.9 kg CO₂/Sm³** · LPG **3.0 t CO₂/t** · Indian grid **0.70 t CO₂/MWh** (= 0.70 kg/kWh) · petrol car 120 g/km · GWP: CO₂ 1, CH₄ ≈ 28, N₂O 273, SF₆ 25,200. If the paper gives a different factor, use the paper's.

**Scope rules in one line:** Scope 1 = **what you burn** (own fuel, own vehicles, process, refrigerant leaks) · Scope 2 = **what you buy** (electricity, steam, heat, cooling) · Scope 3 = **what you influence** (suppliers, hired transport, travel, commuting, use and end-of-life of products: 15 categories).

## Q1 (10 marks). Automotive plant producing ICE cars and EVs (course case) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
A plant makes **50,000 ICE cars** and **20,000 EVs** a year. Scope 1: natural gas 5,00,000 Sm³ (1.9 kg/Sm³), diesel in DG sets 2,00,000 l (2.68 kg/l), LPG 50 t (3.0 t/t). Scope 2: 25,000 MWh of grid electricity at 0.70 t/MWh. Scope 3: purchased materials 6 t CO₂e per ICE car and 10 t per EV; upstream transport 15,000 t; business travel 500 t; downstream distribution 8,000 t; use phase: lifetime 1.5 lakh km, ICE 120 g/km, EV 0.15 kWh/km on the 0.70 kg/kWh grid.
(a) Compute Scope 1, 2 and 3. (5) (b) Compute the plant total and the share of each scope. (2) (c) Interpret the result and recommend strategy. (3)

**Q1.1 (a) Compute Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3. [5 marks]**
*Scope 1* = 5,00,000 × 1.9 = 9,50,000 kg = **950 t** (natural gas) + 2,00,000 × 2.68 = **536 t** (diesel) + 50 × 3.0 = **150 t** (LPG) = **1,636 t CO₂e**.

*Scope 2* = 25,000 MWh × 0.70 = **17,500 t CO₂e**.

*Scope 3*:
| Category | ICE line | EV line |
|---|---|---|
| Cat 1 Purchased materials | 50,000 × 6 = 3,00,000 t | 20,000 × 10 = 2,00,000 t |
| Cat 4 + 6 + 9 Logistics and travel | 15,000 + 500 + 8,000 = 23,500 t (counted once) | — |
| Cat 11 Use phase | 1,50,000 km × 120 g = 18 t per car × 50,000 = 9,00,000 t | 1,50,000 × 0.15 = 22,500 kWh × 0.70 = 15.75 t per car × 20,000 = 3,15,000 t |
| **Scope 3 total** | **12,23,500 t** | **5,15,000 t** |

Scope 3 for the plant = 12,23,500 + 5,15,000 = **17,38,500 t CO₂e**.

**Q1.2 (b) Plant total and share of each scope. [2 marks]**
Total = 1,636 + 17,500 + 17,38,500 = **17,57,636 t CO₂e**.
Scope 1 = 0.09% · Scope 2 = 1.00% · **Scope 1 + 2 = 1.09%** · **Scope 3 = 98.9%** (use phase alone 69.1%, materials 28.4%).
Per car: ICE line 12,42,636 ÷ 50,000 = **24.85 t**; EV line 5,15,000 ÷ 20,000 = **25.75 t** (before allocating the shared plant emissions).

**Q1.3 (c) Interpret and recommend. [3 marks]**
- **Scope 3 dominates (≈ 99%)**: even a 100% renewable, zero-fuel factory would cut the footprint by only about 1%. The slide's lesson: *Scope 1 + 2 < 5%, Scope 3 > 95%*.
- **EVs are carbon-heavy in manufacturing** (battery materials: 10 t vs 6 t) and **ICE cars are carbon-heavy in use** (18 t vs 15.75 t). On India's coal-heavy grid the EV's advantage almost disappears; on a renewable grid its use phase falls to about 3 t, so the **EV benefit depends on grid cleanliness**.
- **Recommend:** (1) supplier decarbonisation: green steel and aluminium, low-carbon battery cells with recycled cobalt and lithium (biggest lever after the use phase); (2) renewable charging partnerships and efficiency (kWh/km) for the EV line; (3) battery recycling and second life to cut Cat 1 and Cat 12; (4) a solar PPA and electrified heat for Scope 2 and Scope 1; (5) report under the GHG Protocol and BRSR with verified (MRV) Scope 3 data. This is the Toyota/Tesla route the slide points to.

## Q2 (10 marks). Textile spinning mill, full inventory 📗 ⭐⭐⭐
A spinning mill in Tamil Nadu produces **3,500 t of yarn** a year. Data: natural gas in the boiler 4,00,000 Sm³; diesel in DG sets 1,20,000 l; LPG in the canteen 20 t; refrigerant R-410A top-up 40 kg (**GWP 2,088**); grid electricity 14,000 MWh; purchased cotton 4,000 t (**1.8 t CO₂e per t**); inbound road freight by hired trucks 20,00,000 t-km and outbound 30,00,000 t-km (**0.09 kg CO₂e per t-km**); business travel 120 t CO₂e; 600 employees commute 20 km a day for 300 days (**0.12 kg CO₂e per km**). Use the course factors for fuels and grid.
(a) Classify each source by scope. (2) (b) Calculate each scope, the total and the shares. (4) (c) Compute the carbon intensity per tonne of yarn. (1) (d) The mill (i) signs a solar PPA for 50% of its electricity, (ii) switches the boiler to biomass (treated as zero under the GHG Protocol, biogenic CO₂ reported separately), (iii) replaces 30% of cotton with recycled fibre at 0.5 t CO₂e/t and (iv) fixes the refrigerant leak. Compute the new footprint and the % reduction. (3)

**Q2.1 (a) Classify the sources. [2 marks]**
| Source | Scope | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Natural gas boiler, diesel DG sets, LPG canteen | 1 | Fuel burnt in owned equipment |
| R-410A leak | 1 | Fugitive emission |
| Grid electricity | 2 | Purchased energy |
| Purchased cotton | 3 (Cat 1) | Purchased goods |
| Inbound hired trucks | 3 (Cat 4) | Upstream transport, not owned |
| Outbound hired trucks | 3 (Cat 9) | Downstream transport |
| Business travel | 3 (Cat 6) | |
| Employee commuting | 3 (Cat 7) | |

**Q2.2 (b) Calculate each scope, the total and the shares. [4 marks]**
| Source | Working | t CO₂e |
|---|---|---|
| Natural gas | 4,00,000 × 1.9 ÷ 1,000 | 760.0 |
| Diesel | 1,20,000 × 2.68 ÷ 1,000 | 321.6 |
| LPG | 20 × 3.0 | 60.0 |
| R-410A | 40 kg × 2,088 ÷ 1,000 | 83.5 |
| **Scope 1** | | **1,225.1** |
| Grid electricity | 14,000 × 0.70 | **9,800.0** |
| **Scope 2** | | **9,800.0** |
| Cotton | 4,000 × 1.8 | 7,200.0 |
| Inbound freight | 20,00,000 × 0.09 ÷ 1,000 | 180.0 |
| Outbound freight | 30,00,000 × 0.09 ÷ 1,000 | 270.0 |
| Business travel | given | 120.0 |
| Commuting | 600 × 20 × 300 = 36,00,000 km × 0.12 ÷ 1,000 | 432.0 |
| **Scope 3** | | **8,202.0** |
| **Total** | | **19,227.1** |

Shares: Scope 1 = **6.4%**, Scope 2 = **51.0%**, Scope 3 = **42.7%**.

**Q2.3 (c) Carbon intensity. [1 mark]**
19,227.1 ÷ 3,500 = **5.49 t CO₂e per tonne of yarn** (Scope 1 + 2 only: 11,025.1 ÷ 3,500 = 3.15 t/t).

**Q2.4 (d) New footprint after the four projects. [3 marks]**
| Project | Saving (t CO₂e) |
|---|---|
| (i) Solar PPA for 50% of electricity: 50% × 9,800 | 4,900.0 |
| (ii) Boiler to biomass: removes the natural gas line | 760.0 |
| (iii) 30% recycled cotton: 1,200 t × (1.8 − 0.5) | 1,560.0 |
| (iv) Fix the leak | 83.5 |
| **Total saving** | **7,303.5** |

New footprint = 19,227.1 − 7,303.5 = **11,923.6 t CO₂e** → reduction = 7,303.5 ÷ 19,227.1 = **38.0%**. New intensity = **3.41 t per tonne of yarn**.
*Interpretation:* unlike the auto plant, this mill is **electricity-intensive**, so **Scope 2 is the biggest hotspot** (51%) and renewable power is the first lever. Do not assume Scope 3 always dominates: compute it. Report Scope 2 **market-based** (with the PPA) and **location-based** (grid average, still 9,800 t) side by side, as BRSR and the GHG Protocol require. The next lever is supplier engagement on cotton (organic or regenerative cotton, recycled fibre), which matters for EU buyers under CSRD and the Digital Product Passport.

## Q3 (10 marks). Logistics company (3PL), Scopes and an EV-truck decision 📗 ⭐⭐⭐
A 3PL owns **200 diesel trucks**, each running 1,00,000 km a year at **4 km per litre**. Its owned fleet moves 16 crore tonne-km a year. Forklifts use 30 t of LPG. Reefer trucks leak 200 kg of HFC-134a (**GWP 1,430**). Warehouses use 6,000 MWh of grid electricity. It also sub-contracts haulage of 5 crore t-km to other carriers (**0.09 kg CO₂e/t-km**), and staff business travel is 150 t CO₂e.
(a) Compute Scope 1, 2, 3 and the total with shares. (4) (b) Compute the fleet's g CO₂e per t-km. (1) (c) The company plans to replace **50 trucks** with electric trucks using **1.2 kWh per km**. Compute the change in emissions if they charge from the grid and if they charge from a solar PPA, and the grid factor at which the switch breaks even. (3) (d) Recommend. (2)

**Q3.1 (a) Scope 1, 2, 3 and shares. [4 marks]**
- Diesel = 200 × 1,00,000 ÷ 4 = **50,00,000 l** × 2.68 = **13,400 t**. LPG = 30 × 3.0 = **90 t**. HFC = 200 × 1,430 ÷ 1,000 = **286 t**. **Scope 1 = 13,776 t**.
- **Scope 2** = 6,000 × 0.70 = **4,200 t**.
- **Scope 3** = sub-contracted haulage 5,00,00,000 × 0.09 ÷ 1,000 = 4,500 t (Cat 4) + travel 150 t (Cat 6) = **4,650 t**.
- **Total = 22,626 t CO₂e**; shares: Scope 1 **60.9%**, Scope 2 **18.6%**, Scope 3 **20.6%**.
(For a transport company, its own fleet is Scope 1. For its *customers*, the same emissions are Scope 3 Cat 4 or 9.)

**Q3.2 (b) Intensity per tonne-km. [1 mark]**
13,400 t ÷ 16,00,00,000 t-km = 13,40,00,000 g ÷ 16,00,00,000 = **83.8 g CO₂e per t-km**.

**Q3.3 (c) Electric trucks: grid vs solar, and break-even. [3 marks]**
- Km switched = 50 × 1,00,000 = **50,00,000 km**. Diesel avoided = 50,00,000 ÷ 4 = 12,50,000 l × 2.68 = **3,350 t** (Scope 1 falls).
- Electricity needed = 50,00,000 × 1.2 = 60,00,000 kWh = **6,000 MWh**.
- *Grid charging:* 6,000 × 0.70 = **4,200 t** of new Scope 2 → net change = 4,200 − 3,350 = **+850 t (emissions rise by 25%)**.
- *Solar PPA:* about zero Scope 2 (market-based) → net saving = **3,350 t** (14.8% of the total footprint).
- *Break-even grid factor* = 3,350 t ÷ 6,000 MWh = **0.558 t/MWh**. The EV only helps when the electricity is below about 0.56 kg CO₂/kWh.

**Q3.4 (d) Recommendation. [2 marks]**
Buy the e-trucks **only with renewable charging** (solar on warehouse roofs plus a green open-access PPA), otherwise emissions simply move from Scope 1 to Scope 2 and rise. Meanwhile, cut diesel per t-km through load consolidation, fewer empty kilometres, eco-driving and speed governors. Fix the reefer HFC leaks (286 t from 200 kg) and move to low-GWP refrigerants. Ask sub-contractors for their fleet data to replace the default 0.09 factor with primary data. This is Lecture 9's point that *technology ≠ sustainability* and Lecture 11's point that *green transport ≠ sustainable logistics*.

## Q4 (10 marks). GHGs, GWP, carbon neutral vs net zero, credits and CBAM 📗 ⭐⭐
A chemical company emits **40,000 t CO₂**, **50 t CH₄**, **10 t N₂O** and **0.2 t SF₆** a year (GWP: CH₄ 28, N₂O 273, SF₆ 25,200).
(a) Convert to CO₂e and find the share of the SF₆. (3) (b) What would it cost to become "carbon neutral" this year with avoidance credits at ₹900 each? (2) (c) Under a net-zero (SBTi-style) path the company must cut 90% and neutralise only the residual with removal credits at ₹2,500. Compute the residual and its annual cost. (2) (d) It also exports 10,000 t of steel products to the EU. Using an EU carbon price of €80/t, compare the CBAM cost with a default value of 2.5 t CO₂/t and with verified actual emissions of 1.8 t CO₂/t (ignore free allocation and Indian carbon price). (3)

**Q4.1 (a) CO₂e and SF₆ share. [3 marks]**
CO₂ 40,000 × 1 = 40,000 · CH₄ 50 × 28 = 1,400 · N₂O 10 × 273 = 2,730 · SF₆ 0.2 × 25,200 = 5,040 → **total 49,170 t CO₂e**. SF₆ = 5,040 ÷ 49,170 = **10.3%**: just 200 kg of gas causes a tenth of the footprint. Fixing switchgear leaks is the cheapest big win.

**Q4.2 (b) Carbon-neutral cost. [2 marks]**
49,170 credits × ₹900 = ₹4,42,53,000 ≈ **₹4.43 crore** a year. *But* carbon neutral via offsets reduces nothing physically and risks greenwashing claims if the credits are low quality.

**Q4.3 (c) Net-zero residual. [2 marks]**
Residual = 10% × 49,170 = **4,917 t**; cost = 4,917 × ₹2,500 = **₹1.23 crore** a year, *after* investing in the 90% reduction. Net zero = **reduce deeply, then neutralise only the residual with permanent removals**: *"reduction first, offset later."*

**Q4.4 (d) CBAM cost: default vs verified data. [3 marks]**
- Default: 10,000 × 2.5 × €80 = **€20 lakh (€2.0 million)**.
- Verified actual: 10,000 × 1.8 × €80 = **€14.4 lakh (€1.44 million)**.
- Saving from good MRV = **€5.6 lakh a year** (≈ ₹5 crore at ₹90/€). Measured, reported and verified data directly protect export competitiveness; with green steel at, say, 1.0 t CO₂/t the cost would fall to €8 lakh.

## Q5 (10 marks). BRSR data interpretation, TVS Motor (course BRSR report) 📘 ⭐⭐
From the course's TVS Motor BRSR (Principle 6): FY 2023-24 Scope 1 = 22,693.34 t, Scope 2 = 4,437.47 t, Scope 3 = 2,27,00,763.36 t CO₂e ("in progress"); FY 2022-23 Scope 1 = 19,046 t, Scope 2 = 9,584.32 t. FY24 energy: renewable electricity 3,06,833.17 GJ, non-renewable electricity 22,499.84 GJ, fuel 2,99,342.59 GJ, total 6,28,675.60 GJ. The report states Scope 3 of **5.41 t CO₂e per vehicle sold**.
(a) Compute Scope 1 + 2 for both years and the % change; explain why Scope 1 rose. (3) (b) Compute the Scope 3 share and the number of vehicles implied, then Scope 1 + 2 per vehicle. (3) (c) Compute the renewable share of electricity and of total energy, and name the next lever. (2) (d) Which Scope 3 category dominates, and two improvements to the disclosure? (2)

**Q5.1 (a) Scope 1 + 2 trend. [3 marks]**
FY24 = 22,693.34 + 4,437.47 = **27,130.81 t**; FY23 = 19,046 + 9,584.32 = **28,630.32 t** → change = −1,499.51 t = **−5.2%**. Scope 2 fell **53.7%** (more renewable electricity) while Scope 1 rose **19.1%**, partly because the reporting boundary widened (corporate office, test tracks, training institute) and refrigerants are included in Scope 1. Always check the boundary before comparing years.

**Q5.2 (b) Scope 3 share and per-vehicle intensity. [3 marks]**
Scope 3 share = 2,27,00,763 ÷ (2,27,00,763 + 27,131) = **99.88%**. Vehicles implied = 2,27,00,763 ÷ 5.41 ≈ **42 lakh**. Scope 1 + 2 per vehicle = 27,131 t ÷ 41,96,000 ≈ **6.5 kg CO₂e** (≈ 0.01 t), against **5.41 t** of Scope 3 per vehicle: the value chain is about 800 times the factory.

**Q5.3 (c) Renewable share and next lever. [2 marks]**
Renewable share of electricity = 3,06,833 ÷ (3,06,833 + 22,500) = **93.2%**; of total energy = 3,06,833 ÷ 6,28,676 = **48.8%**, because process fuel (2,99,343 GJ) is still fossil. Next lever: **electrify or switch to biofuels** for paint ovens and heat treatment, which would cut the rising Scope 1.

**Q5.4 (d) Dominant category and disclosure improvements. [2 marks]**
**Cat 11, use of sold products** (petrol two-wheeler tailpipe emissions over the vehicle's life), followed by **Cat 1** purchased goods (steel, aluminium, batteries). Improvements: (1) report Scope 3 **by category** with methods and primary vs secondary data, assured under **BRSR Core**; (2) set a science-based target with a baseline year and report EV share of sales as a leading indicator, since EVs move Cat 11 emissions to the (decarbonising) grid.

## Quick checklist before you hand in a CO3 answer ⭐⭐⭐
- Did you **classify** every source before calculating?
- Are all numbers in **t CO₂e**, with kg → t conversions shown?
- Did you apply **GWP** to any non-CO₂ gas (refrigerants, CH₄, N₂O, SF₆)?
- Did you give **shares and intensity**, not only a total?
- Did you name the **hotspot** and give at least two levers with numbers?
- Did you mention **Scope 2 location vs market-based** if there is renewable power, and **MRV / BRSR / GHG Protocol** for reporting?
