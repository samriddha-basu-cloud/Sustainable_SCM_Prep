## 1. From SCM to SSCM ⭐⭐⭐
**Supply Chain Management (SCM)** 📘 is the *co-ordination of material, information and money flows from source to customer*: supplier (point of origin) → manufacturer → warehouse → distributor → retailer → customer (point of consumption). The course's one-liner: *"SCM is why your Maggi, iPhone and Amazon order somehow reach you before your relatives do."*

**Sustainability** 📘 (Brundtland Commission, 1987) is *meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.*

**Sustainable Supply Chain Management (SSCM)** is the management of material, information and capital flows, and of co-operation among companies along the supply chain, while taking goals from **all three dimensions of sustainable development (economic, environmental and social)** into account, derived from customer and stakeholder requirements (Seuring & Müller, 2008, the standard academic definition). The course frames it as **"How to save the planet without killing the P&L."**

### Old thinking vs new reality (Lecture 1) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Old thinking | New reality |
|---|---|
| Cheapest supplier wins | **Ethical supplier wins** |
| Faster delivery | **Lower emissions** |
| Maximum production | **Circular production** |
| Waste is unavoidable | **Waste = design failure** |

The drivers of the change named on the slide: groundwater depletion, fast fashion, food waste, EV battery waste and coal-burning waste. *"This is exactly why supply chains must change with the demanding times."*

### Linear vs circular; cradle-to-grave vs cradle-to-cradle ⭐⭐⭐
- **SCM is "cradle to grave"** 📘: take → make → use → dispose. Responsibility ends when the product is sold or discarded.
- **SSCM is "cradle to cradle"** 📘: products and materials are designed to return to the cycle (reuse, remanufacture, recycle), so end-of-life becomes the start of a new life.
- The course shows SCM moving **from "Linear" to "Circular"** by adding reverse logistics, recycling, returns and circular-economy loops to the forward chain.

### Traditional SCM vs SSCM (Lecture 11 table) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Dimension | Traditional SCM | Sustainable SCM |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Minimise cost, maximise efficiency | Balance economic, environmental and social value |
| Time horizon | Short-term | Long-term |
| Performance | Cost, speed, quality | Plus carbon, water, waste, human rights, resilience |
| Supplier relations | Transactional, price-based | Collaborative, development-based |
| Product flow | Linear | Circular (closed loop) |
| Risk view | Operational disruptions | Climate, regulatory, social, reputational risks too |
| Stakeholders | Shareholders and customers | All stakeholders, including communities and regulators |

## 2. The three pillars (Triple Bottom Line) ⭐⭐⭐
The **Triple Bottom Line** (John Elkington, 1994) says a firm should be measured on **People, Planet and Profit**, not only on profit. Lecture 1 gives the pillar content and Indian examples:

| Pillar | What it covers (slide) | Indian examples (slide) |
|---|---|---|
| **Environmental** (Planet) | Energy savings, water usage, packaging waste, fuel consumption | El Niño, Delhi AQI, Bengaluru floods |
| **Social** (People) | Labour welfare, supplier ethics, safety, diversity | Workers, delivery boys, gig economy |
| **Economic** (Profit) | Carbon emissions (cost), lower waste cost, ESG investments, brand value | Fuel imports, crop damages, water leaks |

*"Switch to sustainable SCM: nature is basically sending in compliance notices."* 📘

The SDGs: the slide's sequence **Denial → Panic → PowerPoint → Real action** describes how firms respond to sustainability, and states that *SSCM is central to achieving the SDG goals* (especially SDG 12 Responsible consumption and production, SDG 13 Climate action, SDG 8 Decent work, SDG 7 Clean energy, SDG 6 Clean water).

## 3. The sustainable supply chain framework (Lecture 1) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐

## 5. SSCM strategy ⭐⭐⭐
### Why strategy must change 📘
*"What happens if your supply chain is profitable today but becomes illegal, expensive or unacceptable tomorrow owing to carbon taxes, CBAM regulations, ESG reporting and customer sustainability audits?"* Supply-chain strategies need to evolve with changing times.

### Definition 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
A **sustainable supply chain strategy** is *a long-term plan that integrates economic, environmental and social objectives (the three pillars) while maintaining competitiveness and profitability.*

### What it adds to traditional priorities 📘
Traditional SC priorities are **cost, quality, speed and innovation**. Sustainability adds **carbon emissions, waste and labour practices**. Because *"in most cases environmental impacts occur across the complete supply chain rather than within the company's own facilities"*, strategy must cover all three emission scopes:
- **Scope 1 — "what you burn"**: fuels and gas (direct).
- **Scope 2 — "what you buy"**: electricity and heat.
- **Scope 3 — "what you influence"**: all other upstream and downstream emissions.

### The SSCM strategy hierarchy and the triple "P" 📘 ⭐⭐
Strategy cascades **corporate strategy → business strategy → supply-chain strategy → operations and logistics strategy**, and at each level it must serve **People, Planet and Profit**. *"Supply chain strategy must support overall business strategy."*

### Why SSCM strategy is important 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Regulatory pressure**: BRSR (SEBI), EU CBAM, carbon markets (India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme), EPR rules, EU CSDDD due-diligence law.
2. **Investor pressure**: ESG ratings drive cost of capital; ESG assets reached about $40.5 trillion in 2020 (Tesla case).
3. **Customer pressure**: B2B customers audit suppliers' sustainability; consumers prefer responsible brands.
4. **Operational benefits**: energy, waste and logistics savings; resilience.

### Ingredients of SSCM strategy 📘 ⭐⭐
Business **vision**, **stakeholder expectations**, **resources**, **capabilities**, **supply-chain design** and **sustainability objectives**. *"Supply chain strategy must aid alignment and add value for business."*

### Risks of ignoring sustainability 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Export barriers** (CBAM, EU deforestation regulation), **carbon taxes**, **supply disruptions** (climate events, water scarcity), **reputation damage**, **investor pressure** and, ultimately, **customer loss**.

### Stakeholder mapping 📘 ⭐⭐
*"Successful supply chains don't just deliver products — they deliver value to stakeholders."*
- **Internal**: employees (safe, fair work), management (performance), shareholders (returns with lower risk).
- **External**: customers (sustainable, affordable products), suppliers (fair terms, long-term partnership), government and regulators (compliance, disclosure), communities (jobs, clean air and water), NGOs and media (transparency, human rights), investors and lenders (ESG performance).
A common tool is the **power–interest grid**: manage closely (high power, high interest: regulators, key customers, investors), keep satisfied (high power, low interest), keep informed (low power, high interest: communities, NGOs), monitor (low, low).

### The five-step strategy development process 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1. **Assess current state** | Carbon footprint, logistics network, supplier performance, waste generation, energy use |
| 2. **Analyse stakeholders** | Who influences, who is impacted, what they expect |
| 3. **Develop future scenarios** | Carbon pricing, supply disruptions, energy-cost increases, water scarcity |
| 4. **Design strategy** | Green sourcing, renewable energy, circular economy, supplier development, network redesign |
| 5. **Operationalise strategy** | Projects, KPIs, budgets, responsibilities |

*"Understand stakeholders → anticipate change → design the response → execute for impact."*

### Sustainability Balanced Scorecard (Lecture 2) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Kaplan & Norton's Balanced Scorecard with sustainability KPIs in each perspective:

| Financial | Customer | Internal process | Learning & growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy savings | Sustainable product sales | Carbon emissions | Employee training |
| Logistics cost reduction | Customer satisfaction | Water usage | Sustainability culture |
| Waste-reduction savings | ESG ratings | Transport efficiency | Innovation projects |
| ROI of sustainability projects | Supplier compliance | Supply-chain management | |

*"Balanced scorecards convert sustainability into action."*

### Carbon management integration and future supply chains 📘
Carbon accounting must cover **all stages** of the chain (sourcing, production, logistics, use, end-of-life). Future sustainable supply chains are **digital, transparent, circular, collaborative, low-carbon and resilient**: *"Future-ready supply chains don't just survive disruption — they turn it into competitive advantage."*

**Lecture 2 key takeaways** 📘: sustainability is a strategic issue, not a compliance issue; SC strategy must align with business strategy; stakeholder expectations shape future competitiveness; balanced scorecards convert sustainability into action; companies that adapt early gain long-term advantage.

## 6. Impact of SSCM on business performance ⭐⭐⭐
This is the syllabus's "impact on business performance" part. Structure your answer by the TBL, or by the levers below.

### Lecture 3: "Which company performs better and is sustainable?" 📘
Company 1: higher sales, higher profits, higher pay scales, **higher debt finance**. Company 2: strong profits, **safe workplaces, lower emissions, ethical suppliers**. The second is better positioned because its performance is durable: it is less exposed to carbon costs, accidents, supplier scandals and financing risk. *"Performance measurement is a core enabler, not an optional one."* and *"If you cannot measure it, you cannot improve it."*

### Traditional vs sustainable performance 📘 ⭐⭐
| Traditional | Sustainable |
|---|---|
| Profit, revenue, cost | Profit **plus** environmental and social impact |
| Short-term, financial | Long-term value creation |
| Shareholders | All stakeholders |
| Lagging financial KPIs | Leading and lagging KPIs across TBL |
| Firm boundary | Whole value chain (Scope 3, supplier ESG) |

### How SSCM improves business performance (with evidence) ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Cost reduction**: energy efficiency, less waste, route optimisation, packaging reduction. Example: Lecture 10's Company B cut energy 25%, CO₂/unit 30%, water/unit 20%, waste-to-landfill 60% → ~₹22 crore/year on illustrative numbers ([CO5 Problem 10](p4-performance-measurement-numericals.md)).
2. **Revenue growth and market access**: green products, eligibility for EU markets under CBAM, preferred-supplier status with customers that have Scope 3 targets (Walmart Project Gigaton, Apple).
3. **Risk reduction and resilience**: diversified, audited suppliers; climate-adaptive sourcing (HUL climate-smart farming); fewer disruptions.
4. **Lower cost of capital**: better ESG ratings, access to green bonds and sustainability-linked loans.
5. **Brand and reputation**: customer loyalty, licence to operate.
6. **Innovation**: new business models (product-as-a-service, battery-as-a-service, remanufacturing).
7. **Talent**: employees prefer purpose-driven employers; safer workplaces raise productivity.
8. **Compliance**: avoiding fines, export barriers and carbon taxes.

### Performance management framework (Lecture 3) 📘 ⭐⭐
**Inputs** (resources, investments) → **processes** (sustainable sourcing, production, logistics) → **outputs** (products, emissions, waste) → **outcomes** (impact on customers, communities, environment) → **business value** (profitability, growth, reputation). *"The framework ensures overall sustainable performance."*

### Performance dimensions Q + D + I + R + S (Lecture 3) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
*"High-performing supply chains integrate Q + D + I + R + S"* across procurement → manufacturing → warehousing → transportation → distribution → customer:

| Quality | Delivery | Innovation | Resilience | Sustainability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Defect rate (PPM, %) | On-time delivery % | Number of new products | Supply disruptions | Carbon emissions |
| Complaints / returns | Order-fill cycle time | R&D spend | Recovery time | Energy consumed |
| Warranty claims | Lead time | Digital technology | Supplier diversity | Water consumed |
| Six Sigma level | Delivery reliability | Patents | Inventory risk | Worker safety |
| Supplier performance | Transport efficiency | Automation level | Business continuity | Ethical sourcing % |

### SMART KPIs (Lecture 3) 📘
Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Slide examples: **economic** — cost per shipment, inventory days, % profitability; **environmental** — CO₂ per tonne-km, % material recycled, water intensity; **social** — accident frequency, % diversity, supplier audit score. *"Measure smarter. Act faster. Perform better."*

### Why measurement matters (Lecture 3) 📘
Performance measurement **drives accountability**, **supports better decision-making**, **builds shareholder trust**, **improves resilience** and **creates competitive advantage**.

## 7. Assignments from the lectures (likely short-answer prompts) 📘 ⭐⭐
### Design a sustainable "chai" supply chain (Lecture 1–2)
- **Tea sourcing**: Rainforest Alliance / Trustea-certified estates, fair wages for pluckers (the Lecture 5 tea-plantation S-LCA example warns that tea can be low-carbon yet socially unsustainable), direct sourcing from small growers.
- **Milk**: local dairy co-operatives (Amul model) to cut transport and spoilage, cold-chain on solar, methane-reducing feed.
- **Packaging**: kulhad (clay) or reusable steel cups, paper instead of plastic sachets, deposit-return for bulk packs.
- **Transport**: consolidated deliveries, EV three-wheelers for last mile, local sourcing radius.
- **Waste disposal**: compost tea leaves, collect cups for reuse, measure waste per cup.

### A medium-size company exports to Europe (Lecture 2)
- **Risks**: CBAM carbon cost on embedded emissions (steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, electricity), EU CSDDD due diligence, EU Deforestation Regulation, Digital Product Passport (from 2026–27 for batteries and textiles), customer audits, loss of orders.
- **Stakeholders that matter most**: EU customers and importers, regulators (EU and Indian), lenders and investors, suppliers (who generate the Scope 3 data).
- **KPIs**: embedded CO₂ per tonne of product, Scope 1 + 2 intensity, % renewable electricity, supplier ESG audit coverage, traceability %, OTIF.
- **Strategy**: measure the product carbon footprint (ISO 14067), switch to RE (PPAs, rooftop solar), green-certify key suppliers, build traceability data, set SBTi-aligned targets, report through BRSR / GRI.

## Quick exam answers (3 marks each) ⭐⭐⭐
**Q1. Define SSCM.**
SSCM is the management of material, information and money flows, and of co-operation among firms along the supply chain, that integrates economic, environmental and social goals derived from stakeholder requirements. It extends SCM from "cradle to grave" to "cradle to cradle": it designs out waste, uses renewable resources and recovers products through reverse logistics, while keeping the chain competitive and profitable ("save the planet without killing the P&L").

**Q2. Differentiate linear and circular supply chains.**
A linear chain follows take–make–use–dispose and ends responsibility at sale (cradle-to-grave). A circular chain keeps materials in use through reuse, repair, remanufacture and recycling, with reverse logistics returning products to production (cradle-to-cradle). Examples: Attero and Lohum recover battery metals; Renault's Refactory remanufactures vehicles.

**Q3. What are the risks of ignoring sustainability in the supply chain?**
Export barriers (CBAM), carbon taxes, supply disruptions from climate and resource scarcity, reputation damage, investor pressure (ESG ratings, cost of capital) and customer loss. A chain profitable today can become "illegal, expensive or unacceptable tomorrow".

**Q4. List the five steps of SSCM strategy development.**
Assess the current state (carbon footprint, network, supplier performance, waste, energy) → analyse stakeholders → develop future scenarios (carbon pricing, disruptions, energy cost, water scarcity) → design the strategy (green sourcing, RE, circularity, supplier development, network redesign) → operationalise it (projects, KPIs, budgets, responsibilities).

**Q5. How does SSCM affect business performance?**
It lowers cost (energy, waste, logistics), opens markets and green-product revenue, reduces regulatory and disruption risk, improves ESG ratings and the cost of capital, strengthens brand and talent, and drives innovation in business models. Measured through TBL KPIs and a sustainability balanced scorecard.
