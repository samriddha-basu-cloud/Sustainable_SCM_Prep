## Section A — Sustainable manufacturing

### 1. The opening question: which company is more profitable after 10 years? 📘 ⭐⭐
| Company A | Company B |
|---|---|
| Lowest cost of production | Adopts renewable energy |
| High pollution | Efficient production |
| High energy consumption | Drives a zero-waste initiative |

Company B, because its costs fall over time (energy, waste, compliance) while Company A's rise with energy prices, carbon taxes, pollution penalties and customer pressure.

### 2. Traditional vs sustainable manufacturing 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Traditional manufacturing | Sustainable manufacturing |
|---|---|
| Maximise output, minimise costs | Balances the Triple Bottom Line (3Ps) |
| High consumption of resources | Optimises resource usage |
| Take–make–dispose (linear) | Adopts the circular economy |
| Compliance-driven, less ESG focus | Innovation-driven, continuous improvement for sustainability |

Focus change: **best cost → best value (economic + environmental + social).**

### 3. Definition (UNEP) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Sustainable manufacturing** is *"manufacturing products using economically sound processes that minimise environmental impacts while conserving energy and natural resources and ensuring employee, community and consumer safety."* (The US EPA uses almost the same wording.)

### 4. The three pillars of sustainable manufacturing 📘 ⭐⭐
| Environmental | Social | Economic |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon reduction, water conservation, renewable energy | Worker safety, ethical sourcing, community development | Productivity, profitability, innovation |
| e.g. rooftop solar, recycling process water, recycling scrap | e.g. employee health & safety, fair wages and skill development, CSR | e.g. lean manufacturing, Industry 4.0, predictive maintenance |

### 5. The sustainable manufacturing framework — "Design · Produce · Use · Recover · Sustain" 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Inputs** — sustainable raw materials, energy, water and other resources.
2. **Manufacturing process** — efficient, clean, safe and value-added production.
3. **Outputs** — quality products with minimal emissions and waste.
4. **Recycling** — collecting and processing end-of-life materials to recover resources.
5. **Reuse** — reusing components or materials in the same or new products.
6. **Remanufacturing** — restoring used products or components to like-new condition.
7. **Closed-loop system** — resources circulate continuously, creating value with zero waste.
*"Closed loop. Zero waste. Sustainable future."*

### 6. Sustainable manufacturing principles with company examples 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Principle | What it means | Example 1 | Example 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lean manufacturing** | Eliminate non-value-adding waste; flow and pull | Toyota: Toyota Production System (TPS), JIT | Danaher: Danaher Business System (DBS) to reduce waste and boost innovation |
| **Six Sigma** | Reduce process variation (DMAIC; 3.4 defects per million) | Motorola developed Six Sigma to reduce variation and improve quality | GE Aviation: optimise processes, reduce defects, improve reliability |
| **Kaizen** | Continuous, small, employee-led improvement | Toyota: all employees pursue continuous gains in quality, safety and efficiency | Canon: improves workflow, product quality and reduces waste |
| **Circular economy** | Keep products and materials in use | Interface (carpets): recovers used carpet tiles, recycles materials, closed-loop remanufacturing | Michelin: recycled and renewable materials, retreads tyres |
| **Cleaner production** | Prevent pollution at source rather than treat it at the end of the pipe | 3M: "3P" (Pollution Prevention Pays) programme to redesign products and processes | Asian Paints: water recycling, cleaner and energy-efficient technologies |
| **Eco-design** | Design for low impact over the life cycle (energy, materials, disassembly) | Philips: energy-efficient, recyclable products, easy to dismantle at end-of-life | IKEA: modular furniture from renewable and recycled materials |
| **Life-cycle thinking** | Consider impacts from cradle to grave (LCA) | Intel: uses LCA to reduce critical-material impacts | Tata Steel: applies LCA to improve resource efficiency and cut emissions |

### 7. The seven manufacturing wastes (TIMWOOD) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Each lean waste is also an environmental waste ("Lean + Green"):

| Waste | Lean meaning | Environmental consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **T**ransport | Unnecessary movement of materials | Fuel, emissions, packaging, damage |
| **I**nventory | Excess raw material, WIP, finished goods | Storage energy, obsolescence, spoilage, scrapping |
| **M**otion | Unnecessary movement of people | Wasted energy, ergonomics and safety risks |
| **W**aiting | Idle time for machines or people | Machines idling and consuming energy |
| **O**ver-production | Producing more or earlier than needed | All the materials, energy and emissions of unneeded products |
| **O**ver-processing | More work or precision than the customer needs | Extra energy, chemicals, materials |
| **D**efects | Rework and scrap | Extra materials, energy, transport and landfill ("quality failure is an environmental failure", Lecture 10) |

Some lists add an eighth waste: **S**kills (unused employee talent) → TIMWOODS.

### 8. Sustainable manufacturing metrics 📘 ⭐⭐
| Environmental | Social | Economic |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon intensity (tCO₂e per unit of revenue or production) | Employee health & safety: LTIFR | Productivity and operational efficiency: **OEE** |
| Energy efficiency and renewable adoption: RE % | Diversity, equity & inclusion: gender-diversity ratio | Cost efficiency: cost per unit |
| Water withdrawal and discharge: water footprint | Human rights and ethical practices: attrition rate | Resource efficiency: material yield %, scrap % |
| Waste and circular economy: waste recycled % | Employee engagement and training: number of hours | Waste and loss reduction |
| Biodiversity; SBTi alignment; GHG (Scope 1, 2, 3); climate risk | Customer data protection; supply-chain audits | Financial performance: operating margin %, ROI |

## Section B — Impact on productivity and organisational performance

### 9. How sustainable manufacturing raises productivity 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Six impacts on the slide: **lower defects, lower energy costs, reduced downtime, better equipment utilisation, better employee morale, higher customer trust.**

### 10. The sustainable manufacturing productivity chain 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
*"Sustainable today, competitive tomorrow."*
1. **Resource efficiency** — optimise the use of materials, energy, water and other resources.
2. **Lower waste** — minimise waste, emissions and pollution at source.
3. **Lower cost** — reduce consumption, disposal and rework costs across the value chain.
4. **Higher productivity** — improve efficiency, reduce downtime and increase output.
5. **Higher profitability** — better margins through efficiency, quality and innovation.
6. **Competitive advantage** — stronger brand, happier customers and long-term success.
*"Efficiency drives profit. Sustainability secures the future."*

### 11. Sustainable manufacturing KPIs 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Environmental | Social | Operational | Financial |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy intensity | Lost-time injury (LTIFR) | **OEE** | Manufacturing cost per unit |
| GHG emissions | Forced-labour violations (%) | **First-pass yield (FPY)** | Energy cost per unit |
| Water intensity | Training hours | Scrap / rejection rate | Cost savings % |
| Waste generation rate | Attrition rate % | Manufacturing cycle time | ROI % |
| Waste recycling rate | Employee satisfaction index | Overall productivity | Revenue from sustainable products |

Formulas: OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality; FPY = good units first time ÷ units started; energy intensity = energy ÷ output. [Worked numbers](p4-performance-measurement-numericals.md).

### 12. The Toyota Way: Lean + JIT + Jidoka + Sustainability 📘 ⭐⭐
- **JIT (just-in-time)**: produce only what is needed, when needed, in the quantity needed → less inventory, less over-production waste.
- **Jidoka (automation with a human touch)**: stop the line at the first defect (andon) → defects are not passed on, so less rework and scrap.
- **Heijunka** (levelled production), **kaizen**, **standard work**, **respect for people**.
- Toyota's Bidadi plant (Karnataka) is the course's reference video for TPS in India; Toyota's Environmental Challenge 2050 extends TPS to zero CO₂ plants.

### 13. Indian case studies (Lecture 7) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Company | Key attributes |
|---|---|
| **Tata Steel** | Circular economy (waste to energy); decarbonisation (alternate fuels, biochar trials); resource efficiency (high material utilisation) |
| **Asian Paints** | Water stewardship (zero liquid discharge); renewable energy about **60% in FY25**; waste reduction (solvent recovery) |
| **Dalmia Cement** | Low-carbon cement (**> 80%** of the mix is low carbon); waste as a resource (~40% recycled as input); renewable energy target **100% by 2030** |
| **M&M Ltd.** | Renewable energy about **37% in FY25**; zero waste achieved in farm-equipment plants; smart manufacturing (digital twins, cobots, automation) |
| **ITC Ltd.** | Triple-bottom-line integration of ESG; "resource positive" (carbon + water + solid waste); **50% RE** and sustainable packaging |
| **Arvind Mills** | Water circularity (**100% recycled water**); recycled/regenerative fibres; cleaner processes (ozone washing, laser finishing) |

### 14. Technology enablers — Industry 4.0 and the smart sustainable factory 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Industry 4.0 — "smarter technologies, intelligent operations, sustainable future"**
1. **AI** — intelligent decisions, big-data analysis to optimise processes, quality and efficiency.
2. **IoT** — connects machines, systems and sensors; real-time data; visibility and agile operations.
3. **Robotics** — automate repetitive and hazardous tasks; precision, speed, consistency; safety.
4. **Digital twins** — virtual replicas of assets and processes; simulate and optimise; reduce risk and speed innovation.
5. **Predictive maintenance** — data and AI predict equipment failures; minimise downtime and cost; extend asset life.
6. **Cloud manufacturing** — on-demand computing; collaboration across the value chain; scalable, cost-effective.

**Smart sustainable factory — "intelligent, connected, sustainable"**: sensors (collect data from machines and environment) → real-time monitoring (operations, performance, energy use, emissions) → AI analytics (patterns, anomalies, improvement opportunities) → predictive maintenance → lower downtime → higher productivity.

### 15. The way forward — future manufacturing: "sustainable, intelligent, resilient" 📘 ⭐⭐
1. **Net-zero factory** — 100% renewable energy, energy efficiency and electrification, net-zero emissions across the value chain.
2. **Circular economy** — design for durability; reuse, repair, remanufacture; zero waste to landfill.
3. **Green hydrogen** — clean fuel for high-temperature processes; decarbonising hard-to-abate sectors; energy independence.
4. **Industrial symbiosis** — collaboration across industries; by-product utilisation; shared resources ("one industry's waste is another's resource", e.g. Kalundborg, Denmark; fly ash from power plants into cement).
5. **Carbon accounting** — measure, monitor and manage Scope 1, 2 and 3; data-driven climate action.
6. **Digital Product Passport** — end-to-end product data transparency; traceability, compliance, circularity.
7. **AI-driven manufacturing** — intelligent automation, real-time optimisation, predictive analytics.

### 16. Key takeaways — Cleaner, Leaner, Smarter, Profitable, Resilient 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- **Cleaner**: carbon emissions, energy and water consumption, pollution and waste.
- **Leaner**: process waste, defects, excess inventory, waiting time and unnecessary movement.
- **Smarter**: AI, IoT, automation, digital twins and real-time data.
- **Profitable**: reduces energy, material, waste, maintenance and operating costs.
- **Resilient**: withstands resource shortages, energy-price volatility, supply disruptions, climate risks and changing regulations.
*"The factories that will dominate the future are not those that produce the most, but those that produce the smartest, cleanest and most efficiently."*

## The textile-factory exercise (Lecture 7) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Problem**: a textile factory has high energy use, high scrap and high water use. Suggest lean, green and digital solutions.

| Problem | Lean solutions | Green solutions | Digital solutions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High energy use** | Eliminate idle running (waiting waste), SMED to cut changeover reheating, TPM to stop steam and air leaks | Rooftop solar and RE PPAs, heat recovery from dyeing effluent, efficient boilers on biomass, VFD motors | IoT energy meters per machine, AI to schedule energy-intensive batches in solar hours, digital twin of the boiler |
| **High scrap** | Jidoka/poka-yoke at cutting, standard work, root-cause (5 Whys, fishbone), Six Sigma DMAIC on shade variation | Recycle offcuts into recycled yarn (Arvind's recycled fibres), design-to-reduce cut waste | AI vision inspection, CAD-based marker making to maximise fabric utilisation, SPC dashboards |
| **High water use** | Right-first-time dyeing (fewer re-dyes), reduce over-processing (unnecessary washes) | Zero liquid discharge and 100% recycled water (Arvind), ozone washing and laser finishing (cut water and chemicals), rainwater harvesting | Smart water meters, leak detection, real-time effluent monitoring |

KPIs to track: kWh per kg of fabric, scrap %, kL water per kg, FPY, OEE, % RE, % water recycled.

## Quick exam answers ⭐⭐⭐
**Q1. Define sustainable manufacturing and give its pillars.**
UNEP: making products with economically sound processes that minimise environmental impacts, conserve energy and natural resources and ensure employee, community and consumer safety. Pillars: environmental (carbon, water, RE), social (safety, ethical sourcing, community), economic (productivity, profitability, innovation).

**Q2. How does sustainable manufacturing improve productivity?**
Through the productivity chain: resource efficiency → lower waste → lower cost → higher productivity → higher profitability → competitive advantage. Concretely: fewer defects, lower energy cost, less downtime, better equipment utilisation (OEE), better morale and higher customer trust.

**Q3. Link lean and green.**
All seven lean wastes (TIMWOOD) consume materials and energy without adding value, so removing them cuts emissions and cost together; e.g. defects → rework energy and scrap, over-production → unneeded materials, transport → fuel.

**Q4. Name four Industry 4.0 enablers and their sustainability benefit.**
IoT (real-time energy and emissions monitoring), AI (optimise processes, reduce waste), digital twins (simulate before building, less trial-and-error), predictive maintenance (less downtime, longer asset life).
