# SSCM Best Practices & Future Strategies
Syllabus topic 10 📘 (Lecture 11: "Developing SSCM strategies — understanding contemporary challenges and future sustainability transitions", plus the best-practice cases spread across Lectures 1–10). This is mainly **CO6** (low weightage, 3-mark theory questions) and is part of the **20-mark online quiz** (PPTs 9, 10, 11). Learn the lists, the frameworks and the one-line "key messages" — quiz MCQs are built from them.

**Opening question (Lecture 11)** 📘: *"If a supply chain is cheap, fast and reliable, but creates excessive carbon emissions, resource depletion or human-rights risks, is it actually a good supply chain?"* **Sustainability is no longer an add-on to SCM; it is becoming a fundamental design constraint.**

---

## Part A — Why supply-chain strategy is changing

### 1. Traditional vs sustainable SCM (Lecture 11) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Traditional SCM | Sustainable SCM |
|---|---|
| Lowest cost | **Life-cycle value** |
| Linear flow | **Circular flow** |
| Supplier price | **Supplier sustainability + resilience** |
| Efficiency | **Efficiency + ecological limits** |
| Scope 1/2 focus | **Full value chain / Scope 3** |
| Waste = cost | **Waste = resource** |
| Forecast demand | **Shape demand + circular business models** |
| Global optimisation | **Resilient, diversified networks** |
**"Sustainable SCM does not mean sacrificing economic performance. It means changing the definition of performance."**

### 2. The supply chain has become the sustainability battlefield 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Where sustainability impacts occur across the chain:
| Stage | Impacts |
|---|---|
| Raw materials | Mining, agriculture, water, biodiversity, human rights |
| Suppliers | Energy, labour, emissions, waste, occupational safety |
| Manufacturing | Energy, materials, chemicals, water, waste |
| Logistics | Transport, warehousing, packaging, fuel |
| Customer | Product use, energy consumption, behaviour |
| End-of-life | Reuse, repair, refurbishment, recycling, disposal |
**Key question: "Where in the value chain is the largest sustainability impact — and who has the power to change it?"**

### 3. The contemporary challenge: a "polycrisis" 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| # | Crisis | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Climate change** | Extreme weather, floods, droughts, heat; physical disruption of facilities and logistics |
| 2 | **Geopolitical fragmentation** | Trade restrictions, sanctions, protectionism, regionalisation |
| 3 | **Critical-resource constraints** | Lithium, copper, nickel, cobalt, rare earths |
| 4 | **Regulatory pressure** | Carbon reporting, due diligence, EPR, product traceability, digital product passports |
| 5 | **Social risks** | Forced labour, unsafe working conditions, living wages, human rights |
| 6 | **Technology disruption** | AI, automation, digital twins, blockchain, new manufacturing technologies |
**Key message: "The sustainable supply chain of 2030 must simultaneously be green and resilient"** (World Economic Forum Global Risks Report 2026).

### 4. The five big sustainability challenges 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Carbon** — decarbonise the entire value chain.
2. **Resources** — do more with less material.
3. **Circularity** — move from take–make–waste to closed loops.
4. **People** — protect workers and communities.
5. **Resilience** — survive disruption without shifting environmental/social costs elsewhere.
**Strategic shift: Efficiency → Resilience → Sustainability → Regeneration.**

### 5. Climate change changes supply-chain design 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
Traditional question: *what is the cheapest location?* New questions: What is the location's **climate risk**? Its **future water availability**? The **carbon intensity of its electricity**? Its exposure to **extreme weather**? How dependent is the network on a **single port**? Can **suppliers decarbonise**? What happens under a **1.5 °C / 2 °C / 3 °C scenario**?
- **Mitigation**: reduce the supply chain's impact on the climate.
- **Adaptation**: reduce the supply chain's vulnerability to climate change.
- **Best strategy = both.**

## Part B — The strategic framework

### 6. The sustainable supply-chain strategy architecture (7 steps) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1. **Diagnose** | Map material, carbon, water, social and risk hotspots |
| 2. **Prioritise** | Identify high-impact / high-risk activities |
| 3. **Redesign** | Products, suppliers, processes, logistics, network |
| 4. **Collaborate** | Suppliers, customers, government, competitors, communities |
| 5. **Measure** | Carbon, circularity, cost, service, social performance |
| 6. **Innovate** | Digitalisation, AI, clean technology, new business models |
| 7. **Scale** | Embed sustainability into procurement, investment and governance |
Memory hook: **"D-P-R-C-M-I-S — Diagnose, Prioritise, Redesign, Collaborate, Measure, Innovate, Scale."**

### 7. Five strategies 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
**Strategy 1 — Sustainable procurement**: a balanced KPI set:
| Dimension | Example KPI |
|---|---|
| Economic | Total cost |
| Environmental | kg CO₂e per unit |
| Energy | Renewable-energy % |
| Resources | Recycled content |
| Water | m³ per unit |
| Social | Safety incidents |
| Human rights | High-risk suppliers assessed |
| Resilience | Single-source dependency |
| Transparency | Traceability % |
**Key message: "Do not outsource sustainability."** A company cannot claim a sustainable supply chain while its purchasing practices (price squeezes, last-minute orders) force suppliers toward unsustainable behaviour.

**Strategy 2 — Sustainable product design**: instead of asking *"How do we recycle this product?"* ask *"How should we design this product so that recycling, repair and reuse become easy?"* Strategic concept: **Design → Supply chain → Use → Recovery**, not Design → Manufacture → Sell → Dispose.

**Strategy 3 — Circular supply chains**: from the **linear model** (extract → make → use → dispose) through the **recycling model** (extract → make → use → recycle) to the **circular model** (reduce → reuse → repair → refurbish → remanufacture → recycle). A circular chain creates **new flows**: forward (supplier → manufacturer → customer) and reverse (customer → collection → sorting → repair/refurbish → remanufacture/recycle → supplier). **Key insight: "Circularity is not simply a waste-management strategy. It is a supply-network redesign strategy."**

**Strategy 4 — Sustainable logistics**: avoid the trap that **green transport ≠ sustainable logistics**; consider packaging, warehousing, inventory, delivery frequency, returns and empty miles. **Strategic principle: "The cleanest kilometre is often the kilometre eliminated."**

**Strategy 5 — Digital sustainable supply chains**:
| Technology | Sustainability use |
|---|---|
| IoT | Real-time energy/material monitoring |
| AI | Demand forecasting + routing + optimisation |
| Digital twin | Simulate network alternatives |
| Blockchain / distributed ledger | Traceability |
| Digital Product Passport | Product-level sustainability information |
Example: **the EU Digital Product Passport framework launched in July 2026** (the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation; batteries first). **"Future supply chain = physical supply chain + digital information layer."**

### 8. Sustainability measurement — from metrics to decisions (Lecture 11) 📘 ⭐⭐
| Economic | Environmental | Social | Resilience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue, total cost, working capital, ROI, net profit, debt ratio | Scope 1–2–3 emissions, energy, water, waste, material | Safety, labour standards, human rights, community impact, diversity | Supplier density, recovery time, inventory buffers, geographic exposure |
**The five tests for a good sustainability KPI: material, balanced, actionable, comparable, credible.**

## Part C — Future sustainability transitions

### 9. The eight transitions — "bigger than going green" 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
| # | From → To |
|---|---|
| 1 | Fossil energy → **renewable / electrified energy** |
| 2 | Virgin materials → **circular materials** |
| 3 | Linear products → **circular products** |
| 4 | Global concentration → **resilient diversification** |
| 5 | Opaque supply chains → **traceable supply chains** |
| 6 | Ownership → **access / service models** |
| 7 | Reactive management → **predictive / AI-enabled management** |
| 8 | Sustainability reporting → **sustainability embedded in decisions** |

### 10. The critical-minerals paradox 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
The transition needs EVs, batteries, solar, wind and grids → high demand for lithium, copper, nickel, cobalt, graphite and rare earths → water, biodiversity and emission impacts plus social and geopolitical risks. IEA 2025: **lithium demand +~30% in 2024; 7% of global copper supply exposed to flood or drought risk in 2024.** (Detail in [ch09](ch09-energy-sector.md).)

### 11. From JIT to JIT + JIC + JIS 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- **JIT** — just in time (lean, low inventory).
- **JIC** — **just in case** (strategic buffers, dual sourcing for resilience).
- **JIS** — **just in sequence** (parts delivered in assembly order; in the sustainability context it is also read as "just sustainable").
- **Old model**: global → concentrated → lowest cost. **Emerging model**: **regional + diversified + digital + circular + low-carbon** (Americas, Europe/Africa, Asia-Pacific hubs).
- **Network design variables**: cost + service + carbon + circularity + risk + social impact.
- **New optimisation question: "What is the lowest total life-cycle cost and risk — not merely the lowest purchase price?"**

### 15. The sustainable supply-chain flywheel 📘 ⭐⭐
**Measure → Prioritise → Redesign → Collaborate → Digitise → Circularise → Decarbonise → Regenerate → Measure again.** Each turn builds data, capability and savings that fund the next turn.

### 16. The 7-step managerial roadmap 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Map** — map the entire supply network.
2. **Measure** — carbon, water, material, social and risk hotspots.
3. **Prioritise** — focus on material impacts rather than everything.
4. **Redesign** — products, suppliers, logistics and network.
5. **Collaborate** — work with suppliers, customers and ecosystem partners.
6. **Digitise** — create traceability and real-time sustainability intelligence.
7. **Transform** — move from incremental improvement to new circular/regenerative business models.
*"The future of SCM is not simply about being greener; it is about redesigning the supply chain so that economic value and sustainability reinforce each other."*

### 17. Key takeaways (Lecture 11) 📘 ⭐⭐⭐
- **Sustainability is a supply-chain design issue.**
- **Scope 3 and supplier ecosystems matter more than the firm's own four walls.**
- **Resilience and sustainability increasingly need to be designed together.**
- **Circularity requires redesign of products, networks and business models — not just recycling.**
- **The future sustainable supply chain will be digital, circular, low-carbon, resilient and increasingly regenerative.**

### 18. Ten best practices to write in any long answer ⭐⭐⭐
1. Measure the full footprint (Scopes 1–3, LCA) with MRV and assurance.
2. Set science-based, time-bound targets and link them to executive pay.
3. Make supplier ESG performance part of selection and volume allocation (not only codes of conduct).
4. Develop suppliers (training, finance, technology transfer) instead of only auditing them.
5. Switch to renewable electricity and electrify heat and transport where the grid allows.
6. Design products for durability, repair, disassembly and recycled content.
7. Redesign the network for fewer km, modal shift, consolidation and a reverse flow.
8. Build traceability (blockchain, IoT, DPP) for high-risk materials.
9. Use balanced scorecards with leading and lagging KPIs and the five tests.
10. Collaborate across the industry (shared recycling, pre-competitive supplier programmes like Gigaton) and report credibly (BRSR Core, GRI, ISSB), avoiding greenwashing.

## Quick exam answers (CO6 3-markers) ⭐⭐⭐
**Q1. What is the polycrisis facing supply chains?**
Six simultaneous pressures: climate change (extreme weather), geopolitical fragmentation (sanctions, protectionism), critical-resource constraints (lithium, cobalt, rare earths), regulatory pressure (carbon reporting, due diligence, EPR, DPP), social risks (forced labour, living wages) and technology disruption (AI, automation). Supply chains of 2030 must be green and resilient at once.

**Q2. Explain "Do not outsource sustainability."**
A firm's footprint and human-rights risks sit mostly with suppliers; if its own buying practices (extreme price pressure, short lead times) push suppliers to cut corners, it cannot claim a sustainable chain. Procurement must use balanced KPIs (cost, CO₂/unit, RE %, recycled content, safety, human rights, single-source dependency, traceability).

**Q3. Distinguish mitigation and adaptation.**
Mitigation reduces the chain's impact on climate (RE, efficiency, modal shift); adaptation reduces its vulnerability to climate change (climate-risk siting, diversified suppliers, buffers). The best strategy does both.

**Q4. What does "JIT + JIC + JIS" mean?**
Lean just-in-time flow combined with just-in-case buffers and dual sourcing for resilience and just-in-sequence delivery, in regional, diversified, digital, circular, low-carbon networks optimised for lowest life-cycle cost and risk, not lowest purchase price.

**Q5. List the eight sustainability transitions.**
Fossil → renewable energy; virgin → circular materials; linear → circular products; global concentration → resilient diversification; opaque → traceable chains; ownership → access/service; reactive → predictive/AI management; reporting → sustainability embedded in decisions.
