# Glossary — Sustainable Supply Chain Terms A–Z
Exam-ready one- or two-line definitions. 📘 = term from your course PPTs, readings or cases; 📗 = standard textbook term added for completeness. Use the flashcards and quiz views to self-test.

## 0–9
- **3P programme** 📘: 3M's "Pollution Prevention Pays" programme to redesign products and processes so pollution is prevented at source.
- **5 tests of a good KPI** 📘: Material, balanced, actionable, comparable, credible (Lectures 10 and 11).
- **7 wastes (TIMWOOD)** 📘: Transport, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Over-production, Over-processing, Defects; each creates cost + energy + material + carbon + time.

## A
- **Activity → Output → Outcome → Impact** 📘: Lecture 10's warning not to confuse doing something (e.g. training suppliers) with proving improvement (lower supplier emissions).
- **Adaptation** 📘: Reducing the supply chain's vulnerability to climate change (climate-risk siting, diversification, buffers).
- **AHP (Analytic Hierarchy Process)** 📘: MCDM method that structures goal → criteria → alternatives and derives weights from pairwise comparisons on a 1–9 scale, with a consistency ratio below 0.10.
- **Allocation** 📗: In LCA, dividing the impacts of a process among co-products by mass, energy or economic value.
- **APMC Act** 📘: State law that confined agricultural trade to mandis; Madhya Pradesh amended it so ITC could buy soybeans outside the mandi through eChoupal.

## B
- **Balanced Scorecard (sustainability)** 📘: Kaplan–Norton's financial, customer, internal-process and learning-and-growth perspectives, each with sustainability KPIs.
- **Battery-as-a-Service (BaaS)** 📘: Product-as-a-service model where the customer rents the battery (e.g. JSW-MG Windsor EV), so the maker keeps control of the battery's materials.
- **Benchmarking** 📘: Comparing performance and practices with industry leaders, competitors, global standards or one's own history to find gaps; "benchmark not to imitate but to outperform self".
- **Biological cycle** 📘: Circular-economy loop in which organic materials are used, composted and regenerated.
- **BRSR** 📘: SEBI's Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report: 9 principles, about 140 parameters, Sections A (general), B (management & process), C (principle-wise performance).
- **BRSR Core** 📘: Subset of assured BRSR KPIs across nine ESG attributes, phased from the top 150 listed companies in FY2023–24 towards 1,000; "don't just report sustainability, prove it".
- **Burden shifting** 📗: Reducing one impact (e.g. CO₂) while increasing another (water, toxicity) or moving it to another life-cycle stage; LCA is designed to detect it.

## C
- **Carbon credit** 📘: Tradable certificate for one tonne of CO₂e avoided, reduced or removed, issued by a registry such as Verra or Gold Standard.
- **Carbon footprint** 📘: Total GHG emissions of a product or organisation expressed in CO₂e; a single-indicator subset of LCA.
- **Carbon intensity** 📘: Emissions per unit of output or revenue, e.g. tCO₂e per tonne or per ₹ crore.
- **Carbon ledger** 📘: Emerging practice of recording emissions like financial transactions, managed by carbon accountants and ESG controllers.
- **Carbon neutral** 📘: Emissions balanced through offsets, regardless of how much has been reduced.
- **Carbon zero** 📘: Near-zero emissions across operations and value chain.
- **Carter–Rogers framework** 📘: SSCM sits at the intersection of environmental, social and economic performance, supported by risk management, transparency, strategy and culture.
- **CBAM** 📘: EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism charging importers for embedded emissions in steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity.
- **CCTS** 📘: India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, operational from 2025, setting intensity targets for energy-intensive sectors and allowing trading of credits.
- **CCUS** 📘: Carbon capture, utilisation and storage, used to neutralise residual emissions.
- **CDP** 📘: Carbon Disclosure Project; investor-backed questionnaires on climate change, water security, forests, plastics and supply chains.
- **Centre of gravity method** 📗: Facility-location method giving Cx = Σ xᵢWᵢ ÷ ΣWᵢ and Cy = Σ yᵢWᵢ ÷ ΣWᵢ as a starting location.
- **Circular economy** 📘: Keeping products, components and materials in productive use and reducing waste through redesign, reuse, repair, remanufacturing and recycling (UNEP).
- **Cleaner production** 📘: Preventing pollution and waste at source rather than treating it at the end of the pipe (e.g. 3M, Asian Paints).
- **Closed-loop supply chain** 📘: Forward flow (supplier → factory → DC → customer) combined with reverse flow (customer → collection → recovery → factory or secondary market).
- **CO₂e** 📘: Carbon-dioxide equivalent; emission × global warming potential.
- **Cradle to cradle** 📘: Design in which end-of-life materials become inputs for new products; the course's description of SSCM.
- **Cradle to gate** 📘: LCA boundary from raw materials to the factory gate.
- **Cradle to grave** 📘: LCA boundary from raw materials through use to disposal; the course's description of traditional SCM.
- **Critical-minerals paradox** 📘: The green transition increases demand for lithium, copper, nickel, cobalt, graphite and rare earths, whose mining brings water, biodiversity, social and geopolitical risks.
- **CSDDD** 📘: EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive, requiring large firms to identify and address human-rights and environmental harms in their value chains.

## D
- **DEA (Data Envelopment Analysis)** 📘: Linear-programming technique measuring the relative efficiency of peer units (suppliers) as outputs over inputs.
- **Decentralised collection** 📘: Reverse network where customers return to local collection centres that feed a regional facility.
- **Delivery-based case** 📘: Lecture 1 category for cases where sustainability comes from a better delivery model (ITC eChoupal, HUL sourcing).
- **Digital Product Passport (DPP)** 📘: Digital identity of a product with verified data on origin, materials, compliance and life cycle; EU framework launched July 2026.
- **Digital twin** 📘: Virtual replica of an asset, process or network used to simulate and optimise before acting.
- **DMAIC** 📗: Six Sigma improvement cycle: Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control.

## E
- **E-LCA** 📘: Environmental life-cycle assessment; "what happens to the environment?"
- **Eco-design** 📘: Designing products to minimise life-cycle impact, e.g. Philips' recyclable, easy-to-dismantle products and IKEA's modular furniture.
- **Ecoinvent** 📘: Widely used secondary LCI database.
- **Emission factor** 📘: Emissions per unit of activity, e.g. 2.68 kg CO₂ per litre of diesel or about 0.70 kg CO₂ per kWh of Indian grid power.
- **Empty km %** 📘: Empty running km ÷ total km; a key green-logistics KPI (18% in the Lecture 10 FMCG case).
- **EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility)** 📘: Policy making producers responsible for collecting and recycling their products and packaging at end of life.
- **ESG** 📘: Environmental, Social and Governance; "the business sustainability scorecard for future business".
- **ESG + RTI** 📘: Lecture 6's supplier evaluation scorecard: Environmental, Social, Governance plus Risk, Technology, Innovation.

## F
- **Factor rating** 📗: Location method scoring candidate sites on weighted qualitative and quantitative factors.
- **Fill rate** 📗: Units (or lines) shipped from stock ÷ units ordered.
- **First-pass yield (FPY)** 📘: Units made right first time ÷ units started.
- **Functional unit** 📘: Quantified function delivered by a product, used as the basis for LCA comparison, e.g. "delivering 1 litre of drinking water"; "the most misunderstood" part of LCA.

## G
- **Gate to gate** 📘: LCA boundary covering a single process or site.
- **GHG Protocol** 📘: WRI–WBCSD corporate accounting standard that introduced Scopes 1, 2 and 3.
- **Global Warming Potential (GWP)** 📘: Warming effect of a gas relative to CO₂ over 100 years (CH₄ ~27–30, N₂O ~273, SF₆ ~25,200).
- **Green hydrogen** 📘: Hydrogen made by electrolysis using renewable power; a lever for hard-to-abate, high-temperature processes.
- **Green logistics** 📘: Designing logistics activities to reduce environmental impact while maintaining service and economic performance.
- **Greenhushing** 📘: Under-communicating genuine sustainability achievements.
- **Greenwashing** 📘: Overstating sustainability performance without adequate evidence ("eco-friendly", "carbon neutral").
- **GRI 305** 📘: Global Reporting Initiative standard on emissions (Scopes 1–3, intensity, reductions, ODS, NOx/SOx).

## H
- **Hybrid collection** 📘: Reverse network of local centres, regional hubs and specialised recovery facilities.
- **Hydrometallurgy** 📘: Battery recycling by acid leaching; recovers lithium, cobalt, nickel and copper.

## I
- **Industrial symbiosis** 📘: Collaboration in which one industry's waste or by-product becomes another's input.
- **Industry 4.0** 📘: AI, IoT, robotics, digital twins, predictive maintenance and cloud manufacturing driving efficiency, sustainability and competitiveness.
- **Integrated management system** 📘: Combined ISO 9001 (quality), ISO 14001 (environment) and ISO 45001 (occupational health & safety).
- **Integrated Reporting** 📘: Reporting framework built on six capitals: financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social & relationship, natural.
- **ISO 14001** 📘: Standard for an environmental management system and continual improvement of environmental performance.
- **ISO 14040/14044** 📘: International standards for LCA principles/framework and requirements/guidelines.
- **ISO 14064** 📘: Standard for quantifying, reporting and verifying organisational GHG emissions.
- **ISSB (IFRS S1–S2)** 📘: Global baseline for investor-relevant sustainability (S1) and climate (S2) disclosures.

## J
- **Jidoka** 📘: Toyota's "automation with a human touch": stop at the first defect so defects are not passed on.
- **JIT + JIC + JIS** 📘: Lecture 11's future model combining just-in-time efficiency, just-in-case buffers and just-in-sequence delivery in resilient networks.

## K
- **Kaizen** 📘: Continuous, incremental improvement by all employees (Toyota, Canon).
- **KPI trap** 📘: Tracking too many KPIs (e.g. 75 + 40 + 30) yet being unable to answer whether the chain is becoming more sustainable.
- **Kraljic matrix** 📗: Supplier segmentation by profit impact and supply risk into strategic, leverage, bottleneck and non-critical items.

## L
- **Lagging indicator** 📘: KPI that tells you what happened (injury rate, actual Scope 2, defect rate).
- **LCA (Life Cycle Assessment)** 📘: Systematic method to evaluate environmental impacts at every stage of a product's life (ISO).
- **LCC (Life Cycle Costing)** 📘: Total cost of ownership over the life cycle rather than purchase price.
- **LCI** 📘: Life Cycle Inventory; phase II of LCA quantifying inputs and outputs from primary and secondary data.
- **LCIA** 📘: Life Cycle Impact Assessment; phase III converting inventory into impact categories.
- **LCSA** 📘: Life Cycle Sustainability Assessment = E-LCA + S-LCA + LCC (Klöpffer 2008).
- **Leading indicator** 📘: KPI that tells you whether you are likely to improve (supplier ESG assessments, training, RE contracts).
- **Lean manufacturing** 📘: Eliminating non-value-adding waste through flow and pull (TPS, Danaher Business System).
- **Load-distance method** 📗: Location method choosing the site with the lowest Σ (load × distance), a proxy for transport cost and CO₂.
- **Location-based Scope 2** 📗: Scope 2 calculated with the average grid emission factor.
- **LTIFR** 📘: Lost-time injury frequency rate = lost-time injuries × 1,000,000 ÷ person-hours worked.

## M
- **Market-based Scope 2** 📗: Scope 2 calculated with the factors of contracts bought (PPAs, RECs, green tariffs).
- **Mitigation** 📘: Reducing the supply chain's impact on the climate.
- **MRV** 📘: Measure, Report, Verify; the foundation of credible carbon accounting and ESG reporting.

## N
- **Net Technology Value** 📘: Gross benefits − adoption cost − lifecycle sustainability cost − risk cost (Lecture 9).
- **Net zero** 📘: Reducing emissions deeply (about 90–95%) and neutralising only residual emissions with removals.
- **Net-zero hierarchy** 📘: Measure → reduce → RE transition → sustainable supply chain → offset unavoidable emissions; "reduction first, offset later".

## O
- **OEE** 📘: Overall equipment effectiveness = availability × performance × quality.
- **OTIF** 📘: On time in full; orders delivered both on time and complete ÷ total orders.

## P
- **Pareto-efficient network** 📘: A network for which no objective (cost, CO₂, recovery) can be improved without worsening another.
- **Perfect order rate** 📘: Orders on time, in full, damage-free and with correct documents ÷ total orders.
- **Polycrisis** 📘: Simultaneous climate, geopolitical, critical-resource, regulatory, social and technology pressures on supply chains.
- **PPA (Power Purchase Agreement)** 📗: Long-term contract to buy electricity, often renewable, from a generator; cuts market-based Scope 2.
- **Primary data** 📘: Data measured directly from the company or its suppliers (meters, bills, production records).
- **Product as a Service (PaaS)** 📘: Selling use or performance instead of ownership (CNH harvester rental, Signify lighting, Furlenco furniture); the maker keeps material value.
- **Product recovery hierarchy** 📗: Reuse → repair → refurbish → remanufacture → recycle → energy recovery → disposal.
- **Pyrometallurgy** 📘: Battery recycling by shredding and smelting at high temperature; energy-intensive.

## R
- **R-strategy hierarchy** 📘: Refuse, reduce, reuse, repair, refurbish, remanufacture, repurpose, recycle (highest to lowest value).
- **Recovery-based case** 📘: Lecture 1 category where sustainability comes from recovering value (EV-battery circular chain).
- **Reduction-based case** 📘: Lecture 1 category where sustainability comes from cutting emissions or resources (Hindustan Zinc, Mahindra, Flipkart).
- **Refurbish** 📘: Restore and update a product to good working condition (e.g. an old sofa).
- **Regenerative agriculture** 📘: Farming that restores soil health (cover cropping, crop rotation, agroforestry, organic fertiliser), promoted by Nestlé, HUL and Unilever.
- **Remanufacture** 📘: Rebuild a used product to like-new condition with a warranty (Tata Motors Prolife, remanufactured alternators).
- **Repurpose** 📘: Use an item for a different purpose (old boots as planters).
- **Resilience (4th pillar)** 📘: Visibility, agility, recovery, response and assurance; added to the TBL in Lecture 9.
- **Reverse logistics** 📘: Managing the movement of products and materials from the customer back toward the supply chain.

## S
- **S-LCA** 📘: Social life-cycle assessment; "what happens to people?" across workers, consumers, society and governance.
- **Samyojak** 📘: Former commission agent who, in ITC eChoupal, sets up kiosks, runs hubs and handles logistics and cash.
- **Sanchalak** 📘: Trusted lead farmer who hosts and runs the eChoupal kiosk and aggregates village trade.
- **SBTi** 📘: Science Based Targets initiative, which validates corporate emission-reduction targets against climate science.
- **SCOR + sustainability** 📘: Plan (carbon-aware planning), Source (supplier score), Make (energy/unit), Deliver (CO₂e/shipment), Return (recovery rate), Enable (ESG data quality).
- **Scope 1** 📘: Direct emissions from owned or controlled sources: "what you burn".
- **Scope 2** 📘: Indirect emissions from purchased electricity, heat, cooling and steam: "what you buy".
- **Scope 3** 📘: All other value-chain emissions in 15 categories: "what you influence"; usually 70–90%+ of the total.
- **Secondary data** 📘: Data from databases, literature and government statistics (e.g. ecoinvent).
- **Second-life battery** 📘: An EV battery repurposed for stationary storage; adaptation costs $3,500 (pack), $6,700 (module), $8,000 (cell) in the Tesla case.
- **Six Sigma** 📘: Motorola's method to reduce process variation (3.4 defects per million opportunities).
- **SMART KPI** 📘: Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound.
- **Supply Chain 4.0** 📘: Pyramid from ERP to automation, cloud, blockchain, IoT, digital twins, analytics/ML and AI/GenAI.
- **Sustainable manufacturing** 📘: UNEP: making products with economically sound processes that minimise environmental impact, conserve energy and resources, and keep employees, communities and consumers safe.
- **Sustainable procurement** 📘: Buying goods and services for whole-life value that benefits the organisation, society and economy while minimising environmental harm (ISO 20400).
- **Sustainable supply chain strategy** 📘: A long-term plan integrating economic, environmental and social objectives while maintaining competitiveness and profitability.
- **Sustainability (Brundtland)** 📘: Meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
- **SSCM** 📘: Sustainable supply chain management; integrating economic, environmental and social goals into material, information and money flows; "saving the planet without killing the P&L".

## T
- **TAS (Technology Adoption Score)** 📘: 25% economic + 25% environmental + 20% social + 15% resilience + 15% strategic fit, each scored 1–5.
- **TCFD** 📘: Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures; four pillars: governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets.
- **TCO (Total Cost of Ownership)** 📘: Purchase price plus maintenance, energy, waste and end-of-life costs; the basis of "lowest TCO = higher value".
- **Technical cycle** 📘: Circular-economy loop for products: use → repair → reuse → remanufacture → recycle → new product.
- **Tech-Sustain framework** 📘: Lecture 9's 7 steps: define problem, baseline, alternatives, lifecycle impact, TBL, unintended consequences, pilot → measure → scale.
- **TOPSIS** 📘: MCDM method ranking options by closeness to the ideal solution and distance from the anti-ideal.
- **Triple Bottom Line (TBL)** 📘: People, Planet, Profit (Elkington, 1994).
- **TTW (Tank-to-Wheel)** 📘: Vehicle-level efficiency from fuel tank or battery to wheels (gasoline 15–27%, diesel 25–32%).

## V
- **Vehicle utilisation** 📘: Load carried ÷ vehicle capacity (by weight or cube); 62% in the Lecture 10 FMCG case.
- **Verra (VCS)** 📘: Leading voluntary carbon-credit registry, alongside Gold Standard, American Carbon Registry and Climate Action Reserve.

## W
- **Waste hierarchy (EU)** 📘: Prevention → preparing for reuse → recycling → recovery → disposal, with "polluter pays" and EPR.
- **Weighted scoring** 📘: Supplier-selection method: total = Σ weight × score; simple and transparent, weights subjective.
- **WTT (Well-to-Tank)** 📘: Efficiency of producing and delivering fuel to the vehicle (petroleum fuels 75–85%, Indian electricity about 20%).
- **WTW (Well-to-Wheel)** 📘: Full fuel-cycle efficiency = WTT × TTW; diesel split hybrid best in the IISc study.

## Z
- **Zero liquid discharge (ZLD)** 📘: Treating and reusing all wastewater so none leaves the plant (Asian Paints).
- **Zero Carbon Project** 📘: Schneider Electric's programme to halve the operational footprint of its top 1,000 suppliers.
